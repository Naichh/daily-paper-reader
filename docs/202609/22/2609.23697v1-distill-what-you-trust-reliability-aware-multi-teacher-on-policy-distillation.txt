## ABSTRACT 

Multi-teacher on-policy distillation allows a student to learn from complementary specialists on its own trajectories. Domain-routed approaches, however, select one teacher per example and keep it fixed throughout the response. This design both depends on labels that mixed training corpora often lack and cannot adapt teacher selection when the expertise required changes within a trajectory. We propose TrustMOPD , which replaces example-level teacher selection with label-free, token-level supervision allocation. At each student-generated prefix, Trust-MOPD uses each specialist’s RL-induced displacement from a shared pre-RL ref-erence as a proxy for local reliability, calibrates these scores across teachers, and constructs a weighted distillation target. Across mathematics, code, and instruc-tion following, TrustMOPD outperforms the strongest label-free baseline, increas-ing the recovery ratio from 54 .4% to 91 .5% on S INGLE CAP and from 54 .5% to 

98 .0% on M ULTI CAP , while approaching label-based MOPD on S INGLE CAP .Randomizing token-level weights independently of the student-generated prefix performs no better than uniform weighting, supporting the importance of condi-tioning supervision on the evolving generation context. 

## 1 INTRODUCTION 

Integrating the complementary capabilities of multiple specialized models into a single student is a fundamental challenge in building broadly capable language models (Wan et al., 2024; Tian et al., 2025). On-policy distillation (OPD) queries a teacher along trajectories generated by the student, providing supervision at states the student actually visits (Lu, 2025; Agarwal et al., 2024; Gu et al., 2024; Ko et al., 2024). Multi-teacher OPD (MOPD) extends this framework to complementary spe-cialists, enabling a student to acquire expertise in mathematical reasoning, code generation, and instruction following within a unified training process (Ma et al., 2026; Gao et al., 2026). With mul-tiple teachers, however, an additional question arises: how should supervision be allocated among teachers at each generation state? Domain-routed MOPD approaches address this question at the example level. As illustrated in Figure 1(a), they select one teacher based on the example’s domain label and keep that choice fixed throughout the response (Ma et al., 2026; Gao et al., 2026). This design relies on two as-sumptions: each example admits a suitable domain assignment, and the selected teacher remains appropriate throughout the trajectory. Neither assumption is assured when labels are unavailable or prompts combine capabilities. For instance, mathematical reasoning may need to satisfy explicit instruction constraints, and stronger reasoning does not necessarily imply better adherence to those constraints (Fu et al., 2026a). The illustrative prompt in Figure 1(a) requires mathematical deriva-tion, code-based verification, and JSON formatting. A mathematics specialist may provide useful guidance during derivation without being equally suited to code verification or output formatting. More fundamentally, a teacher’s global domain expertise does not guarantee local supervision relia-bility at the current generation state (He et al., 2026; Wang et al., 2026b). Global expertise describes what a teacher is good at, whereas local reliability concerns whether its learning signal at the cur-rent state is suitable and worth transferring. Because the capabilities required by a response may change as generation proceeds, the value of supervision from each teacher can vary along the tra-jectory (Huang et al., 2025; Xu et al., 2026; Liu et al., 2026b; Li et al., 2026c). This variation is particularly important in OPD, where teachers are conditioned on student-generated prefixes rather than fixed reference prefixes (Fu et al., 2026b). Their suitability depends on the student’s current context, as compounding errors in multi-turn agents can shift trajectories beyond the teacher’s fa-miliar state distribution and weaken supervision (Li et al., 2026a). Teacher-student disagreement is also insufficient as a reliability measure: a large discrepancy may reflect useful expertise, but it may instead arise from stylistic preferences or other idiosyncratic behavior that the student need not learn (Wang et al., 2026c; Li et al., 2026b). A useful reliability estimate should therefore depend on the current state, be comparable across teachers, and require no domain labels. To meet these requirements, we propose TrustMOPD . As shown in Figure 1(b), TrustMOPD re-places fixed example-level teacher selection with state-dependent supervision allocation. We con-sider a common multi-expert setting in which all teachers are obtained by applying domain-specific reinforcement learning to the same pre-RL model (Gao et al., 2026). Their deviations from this shared reference provide a natural signal for the specialization introduced by reinforcement learn-ing. At each student state, TrustMOPD measures each teacher’s RL displacement from the shared reference and uses its magnitude as a label-free proxy for local supervision reliability. When a teacher’s RL-acquired specialization is relevant to the current context, its behavior tends to deviate more strongly from the reference; when that specialization is not engaged, the teacher tends to re-main closer to the reference. An offline calibration makes these scores comparable across teachers, after which they are normalized into token-level supervision weights (Huang et al., 2025; Xu et al., 2026). This procedure requires no domain labels, router, or auxiliary model. Because the weights sum to one at every token, the form of the distillation loss, the total supervision mass, and the effective update scale remain unchanged; only the allocation of supervision across teachers varies. As summarized in Figure 1(c), we evaluate TrustMOPD using three RL specialists in mathematics, code, and instruction following on our S INGLE CAP and M ULTI CAP datasets, which contain single-capability and multi-capability prompts, respectively. On S INGLE CAP , TrustMOPD increases the recovery ratio from 54 .4% for the strongest label-free baseline to 91 .5% , approaching MOPD with ground-truth example-level routing without requiring domain labels. On M ULTI CAP , most exam-ples combine two capabilities, making a single domain label insufficient to characterize their super-vision needs. In this setting, TrustMOPD increases the recovery ratio from 54 .5% for the strongest 2label-free distillation baseline to 98 .0% . Randomizing token-level allocation independently of the student-generated prefix performs no better than uniform weighting, supporting the importance of conditioning supervision on the evolving generation context. Together, these results support token-level, state-dependent supervision allocation as an effective approach to integrating complementary expertise from multiple teachers.

## 3 TRUST MOPD: R ELIABILITY -A WARE SUPERVISION ALLOCATION 

Standard MOPD uses domain labels to assign each example to a single teacher and retains the resulting one-hot weights throughout the response. TrustMOPD instead estimates each teacher’s 

local reliability at every student state and converts these estimates into state-dependent supervision weights. The resulting weights are used in the standard MOPD distillation objective without requir-ing domain labels, a router, or an auxiliary model. 3.1 RL D ISPLACEMENT AS A PROXY FOR LOCAL RELIABILITY 

The teachers’ shared origin provides the needed signal. Each teacher πk is obtained by domain-specific RL from the same pre-RL policy πref , so its displacement from πref isolates the policy change induced by that RL run. Evaluated at the student’s current state, this displacement indicates how strongly the learned specialization is expressed locally. As illustrated in Figure 2(a), when a teacher’s domain specialization is engaged at the current state, domain RL substantially shifts its next-token preferences away from the reference, yielding a large displacement; otherwise, the teacher remains close to the reference and the displacement is small. Teacher–student divergence lacks this interpretation because it also varies with the student’s capability and training stage. We therefore use RL displacement from the shared reference as a label-free proxy for local reliability. Let qk,t = πk(· | st) and qref ,t = πref (· | st) denote the next-token distributions of teacher k and reference at state st. ## 5 RELATED WORK 

On-policy distillation and adaptive supervision. Knowledge distillation transfers a teacher’s predictive behavior to a student (Hinton et al., 2015; Xu et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2025). For au-toregressive models, sequence-level distillation trains on fixed teacher-generated trajectories (Kim & Rush, 2016), while interactive imitation learning addresses distribution mismatch through learner-induced states (Ross et al., 2011; Lin et al., 2020). OPD applies this principle by supervising student-generated prefixes (Agarwal et al., 2024; Gu et al., 2024; Ko et al., 2024; Lu, 2025). Complementary work improves OPD: SimCT enables cross-tokenizer supervision (Sun et al., 2026a), while Easy-OPD provides a unified framework for heterogeneous OPD methods (Sun et al., 2026b). Subsequent work modifies distillation divergences, token weighting, or target distributions (Wu et al., 2025; Ko et al., 2025; Jin et al., 2026b; Jang et al., 2026; Zhang et al., 2026c). Other studies examine teacher– student compatibility and failure modes of on-policy supervision (Li et al., 2026b; Fu et al., 2026b; Wang et al., 2026b). Adaptive methods select teacher rollouts or gate supervision using rewards and verifiers (Zhang et al., 2026b; Akhondzadeh et al., 2026; Zhang et al., 2026d). Finer-grained approaches identify informative or teachable token positions (Huang et al., 2025; Xu et al., 2026; Wang et al., 2026c), exploit position-dependent reliability or reasoning prefixes (Liu et al., 2026b; Zhang et al., 2026a; Zhu et al., 2026), and combine trajectory filtering with token reweighting (Li et al., 2026c). VA-OPD reweights visual supervision at rollout and token levels (Liu et al., 2026a), while influence-directed distillation suppresses diversity-reducing updates (Yang et al., 2026). Trust-MOPD instead selects which specialists supervise each student-generated state. 

Multi-teacher distillation and capability integration. Multi-teacher distillation combines out-puts or representations from multiple teachers (You et al., 2017; Fukuda et al., 2017; Wu et al., 2021), with later methods learning non-uniform teacher selection across examples or training steps (Yuan et al., 2021; Ding et al., 2024). For LLMs, capability integration has been explored through teacher rationales (Tian et al., 2025; Jin et al., 2026a), distribution matching (Wan et al., 2024; 2025), and multi-agent interaction traces (Chen et al., 2024); parameter-space methods instead combine fine-tuned models through averaging or task-vector composition (Wortsman et al., 2022; Ilharco et al., 2023; Yadav et al., 2023), without explicitly assigning teacher supervision during generation. More closely related, MOPD assigns a domain-selected teacher to each student response (Ma et al., 2026), while Open-MOPD balances optimization budgets and refreshes rewards under oracle routing (Gao et al., 2026). Recent methods further adapt supervision through domain resampling (Sun et al., 2026c), answer-based teacher filtering (He et al., 2026), multi-agent debate and confidence weight-ing (Wang et al., 2026a), confidence-aware teacher selection (Yin et al., 2026), or verifier-gated token-level supervision (Xu & Zhang, 2026). TrustMOPD instead uses RL displacement from a 9shared reference to assign token-level weights among fixed specialists, without domain labels, out-come verifiers, auxiliary routers, or teacher debate. 

## 6 CONCLUSION 

In multi-teacher on-policy distillation (MOPD), a central challenge is to combine specialized teach-ers whose usefulness may change across the states of a student-generated trajectory. We introduce TrustMOPD, a label-free approach that estimates teacher reliability from each teacher’s RL dis-placement relative to a shared pre-RL reference and dynamically allocates supervision at every student state. Across single- and multi-capability settings, TrustMOPD consistently outperforms state-independent alternatives, matches label-informed MOPD when domain labels are available, and achieves the best overall performance among the compared methods on multi-capability data. Further analyses show that its allocations reflect teacher specialization and adapt within individual responses, while its performance remains stable across a range of allocation sharpness values. To-gether, these results establish state-dependent reliability-aware allocation as an effective approach to coordinating specialized teachers in multi-teacher OPD.
