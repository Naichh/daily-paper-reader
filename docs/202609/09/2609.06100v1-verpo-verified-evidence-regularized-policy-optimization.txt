## Abstract 

Verifiable outcome rewards guide language-model post-training, but sequence-level advantages do not identify which token-level decisions should be preserved or revised. Evidence-conditioned Teachers provide denser supervision by replaying sampled trajectories with privileged feedback. Yet indiscriminate imitation risks transferring formatting or reasoning-style shifts that do not support task success. We introduce VERPO , a Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization framework that treats evidence as a proposal for policy correction while retaining the outcome objective. It separates evidence-free reference restoration from signed token-level evidence corrections. Fisher Evidence Contrast attenuates corrections along an estimated evidence-presence direction. A stopped token-wise ZPD controller scales acceptance according to local reward alignment and Fisher movement cost, while the reference channel remains independent of acceptance. Across five scientific-reasoning and tool-use tasks, the best variant on each backbone exceeds the strongest compared baseline in average score. The averages rise from 0.6826 to 0.6857 on Qwen3-4B, from 0.6895 to 0.7058 on Qwen3-8B, and from 0.4751 to 0.5657 on Llama-3.2-1B. 

## 1 Introduction 

Outcome-based policy optimization with verifiable rewards has become a central approach for improving the reasoning behavior of language models (Shao et al., 2024; DeepSeek-AI, 2025; Yu et al., 2025). In reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR), task success can be checked automatically, allowing policy updates to be grounded in reliable outcome-level feedback. Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) (Shao et al., 2024) derives group-relative advantages from multiple on-policy rollouts and uses them to reinforce or suppress complete responses. This signal is attractive because it is directly tied to final task success. However, GRPO-style methods apply the resulting sequence-level advantage uniformly across all response tokens. This coarse credit assignment reinforces or penalizes an entire response without identifying which local decisions should be preserved, reinforced, or revised. Evidence-conditioned self-distillation offers a complementary source of supervision. By replaying sampled trajectories with privileged feedback, a self-Teacher can provide dense token-level guidance without requiring the deployed Student to access that evidence (H¨ ubotter et al., 2026). This addresses the sparsity of outcome rewards, but it introduces a different problem: a Teacher–Student discrepancy is not automatically a task-improving correction. Naively imitating the evidence-conditioned Teacher distribution can transfer evidence-induced artifacts, including formatting, confidence, verbosity, or reasoning-style preferences, rather than only task-relevant improvements (Lazaridis et al., 2026; Harne 1arXiv:2609.06100v1 [cs.LG] 5 Sep 2026 et al., 2026). Such shifts can destabilize learning when they are not aligned with verifiable task success (Kaur et al., 2026; Harne et al., 2026). These observations suggest that the central challenge is not simply how to combine an RL loss with a distillation loss. Rather, a useful method must separate three roles that are usually entangled: the outcome-based criterion that defines task improvement, the evidence-induced direction that proposes a local policy change, and the decision of whether that change is worth accepting at a particular token. This leads to the question: 

Can evidence provide dense guidance for outcome-based optimization while remaining subordinate to the verifiable reward objective? 

We introduce VERPO , a Verified Evidence-Regularized Policy Optimization framework that treats evidence as a proposal for policy correction rather than as a replacement for the outcome objective. VERPO decomposes Teacher guidance into an evidence-free reference term, which preserves reference behavior, and a signed evidence-induced correction, which proposes how the policy should move at each token. After this decomposition, VERPO uses a stopped token-wise controller to decide how much of each correction to accept. The controller compares the correction’s alignment with the local GRPO update direction against its Fisher movement cost, so evidence guidance is strongest where it is reward-aligned and inexpensive to incorporate.

SDPO (H¨ ubotter et al., 2026) provides dense self-distillation guidance without an external Teacher, but does not anchor corrections to verifiable rewards and applies Teacher preferences without reward-verified token-level acceptance. VERPO combines the strengths of both: it preserves the outcome reward signal, obtains dense token-level evidence guidance, and introduces reward-verified, token-wise selective corrections. This comparison highlights that VERPO is not merely a weighted sum of GRPO and self-distillation; it changes the role of evidence from a supervision target into a locally controlled correction proposal. We instantiate VERPO with multiple evidence-direction constructions. The Fix construc-tion (H¨ ubotter et al., 2026) compares an evidence-conditioned Teacher with an evidence-free Teacher, while the Contrastive (CTR) con-struction (Pan et al., 2026) compares Teacher distributions under correct and incorrect evi-dence. We further introduce Fisher Evidence Contrast (FEC), which projects out nuisance shifts associated with the mere presence of priv-ileged evidence. The resulting correction can be used through two update paths: it can scale the 2evidence-distillation loss, modulate the token-level policy advantage, or enter both paths through the same stopped controller. We evaluate VERPO on five scientific-reasoning and tool-use tasks with Qwen3-4B (Yang et al., 2025), Qwen3-8B (Yang et al., 2025), and Llama-3.2-1B (Meta, 2024). The best VERPO variant on each backbone exceeds the strongest baseline with complete noncollapsed results in five-task average. The averages rise from 0.6826 to 0.6857 on Qwen3-4B, from 0.6895 to 0.7058 on Qwen3-8B, and from 0.4751 to 0.5657 on Llama-3.2-1B. These are point estimates without uncertainty across seeds. Diagnostic analyses describe task-dependent acceptance consistent with the controller’s use of local reward alignment and Fisher movement cost. Our contributions are as follows: 

• We formulate evidence-conditioned self-distillation for RLVR as a separation problem, distinguishing evidence-free reference preservation from signed evidence-induced policy correction. 

• We propose a stopped token-wise controller that accepts and scales each evidence correction according to local reward alignment and Fisher movement cost. 

• We instantiate multiple evidence-direction constructions, including Fisher Evidence Contrast, which removes nuisance shifts caused by evidence presence. 

• We report higher five-task averages than GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), SDPO (H¨ ubotter et al., 2026), and SRPO (Li et al., 2026) on three backbones, and analyze when and where token-level corrections are applied. 

## 2 Related Work 

RLVR and fine-grained credit assignment. Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) optimizes language models using automatically checkable outcome rewards. Critic-free methods such as GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) replace traditional actor–critic value estimates with group-relative advantages computed from multiple on-policy responses. DeepSeek-R1 (DeepSeek-AI, 2025) and DAPO (Yu et al., 2025) use related group-relative updates for scalable RLVR. These sequence-level advantages are shared across response tokens, obscuring which local decisions caused the outcome. Process supervision obtains finer-grained signals through human-annotated step verification in Let’s Verify Step by Step (Lightman et al., 2024), automatically constructed labels in Math-Shepherd (Wang et al., 2024), and outcome-derived progress estimates in Rewarding Progress (Setlur et al., 2025). Such approaches require human annotations or automatically generated process targets together with a learned verifier, whose local preferences need not match the verifiable sequence-level objective. VERPO retains the outcome-derived GRPO advantage as the task-level anchor and uses privileged Teacher replay to propose token-level corrections, accepting them only when they align with the local reward direction at justified Fisher movement cost. 

On-policy distillation and self-distillation. Knowledge distillation transfers a teacher’s predictive distribution to a student (Hinton et al., 2015). On-policy distillation reduces off-policy distribution shift by supervising student-generated trajectories with an external teacher (Agarwal et al., 2023). MiniLLM develops a related distribution-matching formulation for language-model distillation (Gu et al., 2023). OPSD removes the external teacher by conditioning the same model on privileged solutions (Zhao et al., 2026a), and SDPO constructs a feedback-conditioned self-teacher for dense token-level supervision (H¨ ubotter et al., 2026). SRPO (Li et al., 2026) anchors self-distillation to verifiable outcomes through sample-level routing. EviSD (Xie et al., 2026) applies reward-anchored modulation to evidence-conditioned search actions. These methods do not jointly separate evidence-free reference retention from signed evidence-induced change, and they do not decide whether an individual correction is worth applying at its local policy cost. VERPO addresses this control gap with a stopped token-wise ZPD controller that accepts and scales each correction according to its alignment with the local GRPO direction and its Fisher movement cost. 

Selective and contrastive privileged supervision. Empirical studies show that privileged super-vision is not uniformly reliable across rollouts or tokens (Armandpour et al., 2026; Harne et al., 2026; Lazaridis et al., 2026; Zhao et al., 2026b; Kaur et al., 2026; Jiang et al., 2026). Teacher 3guidance is better aligned on incorrect than on already-correct trajectories (Armandpour et al., 2026), while privileged conditioning can bias the Teacher toward a particular reference trajectory rather than toward correctness in general (Harne et al., 2026), induce behavior unrelated to the intended correction (Lazaridis et al., 2026), overwrite valid reasoning prefixes (Zhao et al., 2026b), suppress verification and backtracking in thinking models (Kaur et al., 2026), or amplify weakly input-grounded signals (Jiang et al., 2026). CEPO (Heakl et al., 2026) exploits correct-wrong evidence for contrastive token credit, while OCSD (Yang et al., 2026) uses structurally matched privileged and ablated replay views to remove replay-scaffold effects in agentic RL. These contrastive constructions reduce shared effects between privileged branches, but a Teacher difference can still contain a component caused by the mere presence of privileged evidence. Such a component can leak evidence-specific style, confidence, or formatting into the Student without improving task success. VERPO’s Fisher Evidence Contrast (FEC) estimates this evidence-presence direction and projects it out of the task contrast under the local Fisher metric, reducing privileged-context leakage before the resulting correction is applied.

## 4 Experiments 

Models and Implementation Details. We used the instruction-tuned Qwen3-4B (Yang et al., 2025), Qwen3-8B (Yang et al., 2025), and Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct (Meta, 2024) checkpoints. The main benchmark profile used 200 optimizer steps on 8 × NVIDIA A800 GPUs. The separate Llama compute study used two A800 GPUs, as specified in Appendix G. Appendix F provides the training stack, shared hyperparameters, decoding settings, settings for the baselines and VERPO, and prompt templates. 

Datasets. We follow the evaluation setup of SDPO (H¨ ubotter et al., 2026) and consider five benchmarks: Chemistry, Physics, Biology, Materials, and Tool Use. The first four are science question-answering tasks built from the reasoning subsets of SciKnowEval (Feng et al., 2024) and target undergraduate-level scientific reasoning in different domains. Tool Use evaluates whether the model can map a user request and a tool specification to the correct tool call, using ToolAlpaca (Tang et al., 2023). 

Baselines. We compare against GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), which learns from group-relative outcome rewards, and SDPO (H¨ ubotter et al., 2026), which obtains dense token supervision from a feedback-conditioned Teacher. SRPO (Li et al., 2026) routes successful samples to the former objective and unsuccessful samples to the latter. The main table reports two VERPO variants. VERPO-LW applies the ZPD weights to the evidence loss, while VERPO-AM uses them to modulate the GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) advantages. Appendix D.1 reports VERPO-LW+AM, which activates both paths. ## 6 Conclusion 

In this work, we introduced VERPO, a framework for combining verifiable outcome rewards with dense evidence-conditioned supervision without treating every Teacher proposal as uniformly reliable. VERPO separates evidence-free reference restoration from signed evidence-induced correction, uses FEC to remove the measured evidence-presence component, and applies a token-wise ZPD controller to accept corrections according to their alignment with the local GRPO direction and their Fisher movement cost. Across five benchmarks and three model backbones, VERPO achieves the best average performance within each model block. The ablations further show that stability depends on the Teacher update rule, evidence construction, and KL geometry, while the fine-grained analysis indicates that accepted corrections are concentrated primarily in intermediate reasoning rather than final-answer tokens. Overall, these results support treating privileged evidence as a selective proposal for policy improvement, with verifiable outcomes remaining the optimization anchor.

candidates from the Teacher branches used by each correction and adds the sampled token. FKL FEC uses the three branches 𝑞 +, 𝑞 − , and 𝑞 0. The RKL correction additionally includes the Actor’s top-𝐾 

candidates. Thus 𝐾 = 128 specifies candidates per branch, not the size of the final union. Selected log probabilities retain their full-vocabulary softmax normalization. The omitted tail is neither renormalized into the selected coordinates nor represented by an extra bucket. Evidence losses, Fisher moments, and projection statistics on this support are therefore approximations to the full-vocabulary quantities in Appendix C. In particular, a truncated FKL displacement need not sum to zero on the selected coordinates, so the exact full-vocabulary logit identities cannot be assumed unchanged after truncation. Support selection, Teacher values, and projection coefficients are stopped during Actor differentiation, while the Actor softmax normalizer remains live. Appendix D.5 gives 34 the exact truncated logit gradient and measures probability retention for 𝐾 = 128 over 200 training steps. The EMA decay of 0.95 weights the previous Teacher state in Equation (B.7), leaving an update rate of 0.05. This distinction avoids interpreting decay and update rate as the same parameter.
