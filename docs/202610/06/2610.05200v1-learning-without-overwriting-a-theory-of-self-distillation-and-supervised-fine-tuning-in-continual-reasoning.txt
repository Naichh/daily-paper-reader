Abstract 

On-policy self-distillation (OPSD) of large language models (LLMs) has demonstrated the ability to improve reasoning capabilities while preserving previously acquired knowledge. Despite substantial empirical success, the dynamics of OPSD in continual reasoning remain incompletely understood. Mod-eling LLM reasoning as search over a directed acyclic graph, we provide a unified theoretical analysis of both the dynamics of post-training—OPSD and supervised fine-tuning (SFT) in continual learning—and the impact of pre-training on subsequent performance. Our findings establish three key insights with an optimization guarantee: (i) OPSD with hints from correct outputs enables continual learning without for-getting by sparse yet effective gradient descent updates induced by the hint structure. (ii) SFT on correct reasoning paths can lead to catastrophic forgetting due to dense updates along the training paths, which overwrite the information previously acquired. (iii) Diversity in pre-training is crucial for enabling a post-trained model to reach a correct output when a rollout starts from an intermediate state. Our results, supported by theoretical analysis, show that reliable continual reasoning depends on how post-training updates interact with the reasoning structure established during pre-training. 

# 1 Introduction 

Post-training has substantially improved the task-solving capabilities of large language models through su-pervised fine-tuning (Chung et al., 2022; Luo et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2024) and reinforcement fine-tuning (Guo et al., 2025; Jin et al., 2025; Zeng et al., 2025). A central distinction between common implementa-tions lies in the source of training trajectories: off-policy SFT learns from fixed demonstrations (Zhao et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2023), whereas on-policy reinforcement learning (RL) uses reward feedback on trajec-tories sampled from the current model (Wang et al., 2023d; Zhao et al., 2026a). In the latter, the generation distribution determines which experiences are encountered during training, while itself evolving through subsequent updates (Kaufmann et al., 2025; Li et al., 2026). The distribution inherited from pre-training therefore influences which reasoning paths are sampled: even models with the same initial task accuracy can distribute probability differently across paths and risk missing some useful reasoning steps. Using a Marko-vian model of reasoning, Bu et al. (2025) showed that RL fine-tuning can favor high-probability paths while suppressing rare but useful ones. For SFT, preserving existing capabilities is a central challenge: sequential fine-tuning can cause catastrophic forgetting of earlier tasks and degrade general capabilities (Wang et al., 2023b; Li et al., 2024). Efforts to mitigate this problem include learning successive tasks in orthogonal 1arXiv:2610.05200v1 [cs.LG] 4 Oct 2026 low-rank subspaces (Wang et al., 2023a) and applying sharpness-aware minimization to favor flatter loss landscapes (Li et al., 2024). Furthermore, in code instruction tuning, Biderman et al. (2024) reported both reduced forgetting and better preservation of output diversity with LoRA than with full fine-tuning. Self-distillation (SD) has also been explored as a means of learning new tasks while preserving previously acquired capabilities. In recent on-policy variants, the student generates a trajectory, and the same model— conditioned on an expert demonstration or informative feedback unavailable to the deployed student—acts as a teacher. By deriving supervision from the model’s own behavior, self-distillation can reduce forgetting during adaptation (Yang et al., 2024; Shenfeld et al., 2026a). They also provide token-level supervision on student rollouts, with reported improvements in reasoning accuracy over off-policy SFT and training efficiency over GRPO (Hübotter et al., 2026; Zhao et al., 2026b). These methods leverage the pre-trained model’s in-context learning abilities (Shenfeld et al., 2026a; Hübotter et al., 2026), although SD can under-perform RL in some settings (Yang et al., 2026; Kim et al., 2026). Their reliance on student trajectories also makes the diversity of the pre-trained policy relevant to learning. Despite empirical successes, the dynamics of SD and their dependence on the pre-trained policy remain only partially understood. Wang et al. (2026a), the general analysis of self-improvement, established high-probability guarantees for true-risk improvement and finite-sample generalization under self-modification. Existing analyses for self-distillation characterize specific aspects of post-training. Using a Gaussian mix-ture model, Balasubramanian and Kasiviswanathan (2026) showed that SFT on new-task data alone drives the mixture weight associated with previously learned behavior to zero. For autoregressive models, Sang et al. (2026) bounded policy drift within each fixed-teacher window in terms of the distillation loss and the shift induced by a conciseness instruction. However, these analyses directly assume the teacher distribu-tion rather than derive it from hint information. They consequently leave unresolved how hints—unique to self-distillation—shape learning dynamics, which is more difficult because hint-based analysis requires ob-servations of the teacher behavior and its effect on the evolving student policy through multi-step reasoning. Our objective is, therefore, to explain the difference in forgetting between on-policy SD and off-policy SFT, while considering their ability to learn new tasks. We model Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning as a prob-lem of reaching a task-specific goal in a DAG. This model links the sparsity of hint information to localized updates and retention, and provides a quantitative comparison with SFT. We evaluate this comparison nu-merically in Section 4. Although existing studies suggest that pre-training is important for the successful SD, there are few theoretical analyses on its role in on-policy SD. Hence, as a complementary theoretical discussion, we examine in Section 3.3 how limited diversity in the pre-training can leave relevant transitions unlearned and thereby impair reasoning from intermediate states. 

Summary of contributions Our contributions are summarized as follows: • We model the continual learning scenario in Section 2 with a graph-based approach, which is often used to explain LLM reasoning process such as CoT reasoning (Abbe et al. (2024); Yang et al. (2025); Kim et al. (2025)). In particular, we introduce a path-conflict setting to analyze the interference between tasks. • We prove the learnability of a single task under both SD and SFT in Section 3.1 (Theorems 1 and 2, respectively). Notably, Lemma 4 in Appendix B.2 shows that SD with a hint in Equation 9 can realize a sparse update that preserves the performance of the pre-trained model. Furthermore, we show that SD can learn new tasks while preserving previously acquired capabilities, whereas SFT can forget them in Theorem 3 and Theorem 4. These results are aligned with the empirical findings of Shenfeld et al. (2026a). 2• We conduct numerical experiments in Section 4 to support our theoretical bounds. The results confirm that tracing the hint-based sparse updates of SD and the overwriting properties of SFT is helpful to explain the observed learning dynamics. • As a complementary theoretical analysis, we study the effect of pre-training diversity on reasoning from intermediate states in Section 3.3 (Proposition 1). We show that limited coverage in the pre-trained policy can leave relevant transitions unlearned even after SD succeeds from the original start. These results explain how the sparsity of hint information translates into localized updates and retention in continual reasoning. The complementary pre-training analysis gives a theoretical implication for reasoning from intermediate states. 

1.1 Related work 

In the following, we briefly discuss related work on self-distillation and diversity in LLM reasoning. Addi-tional related work is discussed in Appendix A. 

Self-distillation for LLMs. Recent methods differ in the information supplied to the self-teacher. Shen-feld et al. (2026a) introduced SDFT, conditioning the teacher on pre-existing expert demonstrations to su-pervise student-generated trajectories. Their experiments demonstrated new-task acquisition with reduced forgetting relative to SFT. Hübotter et al. (2026) instead conditioned the SDPO teacher on feedback ob-tained after evaluating the student’s own attempts, such as execution errors, improving reasoning accuracy and training efficiency. Zhao et al. (2026b) proposed OPSD, providing the teacher with reference answers or verified reasoning traces, and demonstrated improved mathematical reasoning with greater token effi-ciency than GRPO. Ye et al. (2026) introduced OPCD, including a self-distillation setting, using knowledge extracted from past solution traces or optimized system prompts as teacher context. Despite substantial empirical evidence, the learning dynamics of SD remain theoretically underexplored. 

Diversity in LLM reasoning. Wang et al. (2023c) demonstrated that aggregating answers across diverse reasoning paths improves reasoning accuracy with numerical experiments. Yao et al. (2025) proposed diversity-aware policy optimization, which promotes token-level diversity selectively on correct responses. Their experiments showed improvements in both solution diversity and performance on mathematical rea-soning benchmarks. Using a Markovian model, Bu et al. (2025) theoretically showed that post-training can suppress rare but useful reasoning paths and emphasize the role of exploration in preserving rare but useful reasoning paths already present in the pre-trained model.

3.3 A theoretical implication for pre-training diversity 

The preceding results concern new-task learning and retention from each task’s original start. As a com-plementary theoretical discussion, we now ask when the learned policy can also reason successfully from intermediate states. The role of the pre-trained model in SD (Shenfeld et al., 2026a; Hübotter et al., 2026) and the benefits of diverse reasoning paths (Wang et al., 2023c) motivate this question. We examine diversity through the coverage of reasoning states in the pre-trained policy: because SD updates depend on visited states, limited coverage can leave some transitions unlearned that are needed for intermediate restarts. We fix a task Dℓ and run the same SD procedure from its original start; restarts are used only for evaluation. Under the coverage condition of Assumption 2, every correct boundary receives training opportunities and 9they suffice to support all relevant starts simultaneously (Proposition 3 in Appendix B.8). To overcome this issue, self-distillation requires an amount of training that grows at least linearly with M ′.The gap in success probabilities between the original start and the downstream cluster suggests that limited diversity can pose risks at inference time that are difficult to detect during training. Improved performance from the original start may not translate into better performance when restarting from downstream clusters. Pretrained coverage therefore supports intermediate restartability beyond original-start accuracy. 

Remark 3 (Separation of training times) . Under Assumptions 1 and 2, the model trained by SD can re-sume from any relevant start for a training length satisfying Equation 20 (Proposition 3 in Appendix B.8). Holding other quantities fixed, the nonvisit horizon grows linearly with M ′. If the nonvisit horizon grows asymptotically faster than the sufficient learning scale, there is a range of training lengths for which the two settings exhibit different restart behavior. Under the full-coverage condition, the trained model can reason successfully from every relevant start, whereas under the rare-entrance condition, the cluster beyond the rare entrance remains unlearned.

# 5 Conclusion 

We analyze continual learning under SD and SFT using a CoT reasoning graph. Our theory shows how the sparsity of hint information localizes SD updates to preserve previously acquired capabilities, whereas dense SFT updates along the teacher paths can cause forgetting. As a complementary theoretical implication, we show that limited coverage in the pre-trained policy can prevent successful reasoning from intermediate states even after SD succeeds from the original start. 

Future work. The major improvement is to extend the analysis from the current outcome reward format such as Equation 9 to that of the process reward. As shown in Hübotter et al. (2026), the self-teacher in on-policy self-distillation can act as an implicit process reward model through retrospection when provided with rich feedback. In this section, we discuss additional related work. 

On-policy versus off-policy learning. A central focus of this paragraph is the preservation of previously acquired capabilities. Continual SFT can degrade existing knowledge and reasoning abilities through catas-trophic forgetting (Luo et al., 2023). Recent comparisons report that RL often preserves prior capabilities better than SFT at comparable new-task performance (Lai et al., 2025; Shenfeld et al., 2026b; Chen et al., 2025). Controlled experiments further identify on-policy sampling as an important contributor to this reten-tion advantage (Chen et al., 2025). Zhu et al. (2026) attribute the contrasting forgetting behavior of SFT and RL to inter-task interference: SFT exhibits substantial conflicts during sequential training, whereas RL in-duces less interferences that allow multiple task capabilities to coexist. From a complementary distributional perspective, Shenfeld et al. (2026b) argue that standard SFT fits external demonstrations without explicitly accounting for proximity to the base model, whereas on-policy updates are biased toward solutions closer to the initial policy, helping explain their reduced forgetting. Building on Zhu et al. (2026)’s task-interference perspective, we develop more theoretical analysis of how SFT and on-policy SD differ in learning new tasks and preserving previously acquired capabilities. 

CoT reasoning as graph search. Graph reasoning is a broadly accepted approach for analyzing the rea-soning capabilities of language models. Sanford et al. (2024) characterize Transformer expressivity on graph reasoning tasks in terms of depth, width, and additional computation tokens, and empirically evaluate their theoretical predictions on GraphQA. Bachmann and Nagarajan (2024) use path-star graphs to demon-strate how teacher-forced next-token prediction can favor shortcuts that hinder learning to plan. Kim et al. (2025) model CoT as a metastable Markov process on clustered graphs and establish benefits of search, RL, and distillation for crossing rare inter-cluster transitions. Wei and Kim (2026) study backtracking in graph pathfinding and prove an exponential inference-time advantage of RLVR over SFT when SFT is trained only on golden shortest paths without negative examples. Our work uses a CoT graph model to analyze how SFT and self-distillation affect the retention of what they learned.
