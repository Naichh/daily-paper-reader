SLOW-FAST MULTI-TEACHER ON-POLICY DISTILLATION
FOR CAPABILITY PRESERVATION
Xiaofei Yin1,†, Tong Chu1,†, Jiyuan Fu2, Jun Lan1, Shuheng Zhou1,*, Huijia Zhu1,*
1Ant Security Lab, Ant Group, Shanghai, China 2Fudan University, Shanghai, China
ABSTRACT
Foundation multimodal large language models are designed
to support a broad spectrum of capabilities across diverse do-
mains. Multi-teacher on-policy distillation (MOPD) provides
an effective framework for consolidating domain-specific
expertise into a single student model. However, MOPD train-
ing gradually drives the student away from its initialization
model, and general capabilities decline as the displacement
grows, resulting in capability interference.
A direct rem-
edy is constraining the student toward its initialization, but
this suppresses the acquisition of domain expertise as well.
We propose Slow-Fast Multi-Teacher On-Policy Distillation
(SF-MOPD), which couples a fast model, the current student
updated directly by each teacher, with a slow model, an ex-
ponential moving average of the student. The slow model
absorbs the learning signal gradually, serving as a moving
capability reference that fuses the general foundation with
confirmed domain expertise.
For each teacher, SF-MOPD
computes the teacher-induced update in log-probability space
and removes only the component that pushes the fast model
further away from the slow model, while retaining aligned
and orthogonal components.
Experiments across multiple
model scales demonstrate that SF-MOPD effectively mit-
igates capability interference, enhances specialized multi-
modal capabilities, and reduces the average degradation on
general-capability benchmarks, consistently outperforming
vanilla MOPD.
Index Terms— Multimodal Large Language Models,
Multi-Teacher Distillation, On-Policy Distillation
1. INTRODUCTION
Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) have demon-
strated strong capabilities across diverse domains [1], com-
monly developed through supervised fine-tuning and rein-
forcement learning [1]. Multi-teacher on-policy distillation
(MOPD) [2] has emerged as an effective approach to con-
solidating complementary expertise from multiple domain-
specific experts into a single student, yet gradually drives the
student away from its initialization, a phenomenon we refer
to as capability interference.

A natural remedy is to anchor the student to its initial-

capability erosion, as penalizing the deviation suppresses
both. We instead propose Slow-Fast Multi-Teacher On-Policy

current student updated directly by each teacher, with a slow
model, an exponential moving average (EMA) of the student
that starts from the same initialization and absorbs learning
signals gradually so that recurring directions survive while
transient fluctuations are smoothed away, serving as a moving
capability reference. For each on-policy sample, SF-MOPD
removes only the component of each teacher-induced update
that enlarges the slow-fast displacement, retaining aligned
and orthogonal components that may encode complementary
knowledge. The delivered model is the fast model, which
retains the full learning signal from every teacher while being
kept from drifting beyond its confirmed capability state.
SF-MOPD introduces minimal overhead, requiring no
teacher-specific gradient storage or additional trainable pa-
rameters, and extensive experiments across multiple model
scales and specialized multimodal domains show that it con-
sistently enhances expert-specific capabilities while better
preserving general multimodal competence, outperforming
vanilla MOPD. Our main contributions are as follows: (1) A
slow-fast capability reference built on an EMA of the student,
starting from the initialization and gradually absorbing con-
firmed domain expertise. (2) A per-teacher projection mech-

2.4. Overall Objective
We retain the original MOPD loss as a regularization term,
yielding the overall SF-MOPD objective:

teacher-induced update along the slow-fast constraint direc-
tion. Together, the two terms allow the fast model to absorb
new domain-specific capabilities while remaining close to the
capability state confirmed by the slow model.
3. EXPERIMENT
3.1. Experiment Setting
Datasets: DeepVision-103K [8] is a diverse dataset spanning
multiple visual domains. We partition it by domain capability
to obtain three training sets: DataChart, RealWorld Item, and
Schematic Diagram. To ensure sufficient heterogeneity for
multi-teacher distillation, we additionally introduce Vision-
OPD-6K [9] as the fine-grained visual perception set. For
each domain, we randomly sample a subset of the correspond-
ing training data. To measure domain-specific capabilities,
we use MathVerse [10], VSTAR [11] and ZoomBench [12]
alongside the in-domain benchmarks.
To measure general
capability retention, we further include MMMU-Pro [13],
MMBench-CN [14], MMBench-EN [14], MMStar [15], and
HallusionBench [16] for evaluation.
Setting: For the first three domains, we employ GSPO [17]
to train the respective expert teacher models. For the per-
ception expert, we follow the data construction methodology
of Vision-OPD [9] and use the Vision-OPD-6K dataset as its
training data. We use the Qwen3-VL-8B expert models as
teachers and distill their knowledge into student models of
8B, 4B, and 2B scales, respectively. For evaluation, we first
apply rule-based matching to extract model answers, and for
cases where rule-based matching fails, we employ an LLM-
as-judge strategy using Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct to determine
the correctness of the responses.
Hyperparameters: We use the optimizer AdamW, learning
rate 1 × 10−5, batch size 64, and training one epoch for all
compared methods at each model scale. The EMA smoothing
coefficient is set to α = 0.999 for the all models. The coef-
ficient of the original MOPD regularization term is β = 1.0
for the 2B and 4B models and β = 0.1 for the 8B model. For
numerical stability, we use a ridge term of λ = 10−3 in the
projection operation. All teacher models are assigned equal
weights and we report the average accuracy.
3.2. Analysis
3.2.1. Main Results
Table 1 compares three model scales across 11 benchmarks.
At the 2B scale, SF-MOPD improves the domain-specific av-
erage from 38.4% of the instruct baseline to 44.1%, while
maintaining general capability at 66.4%. At the 4B scale,
it improves the domain-specific average from 54.2% of the
instruct baseline to 56.9%, outperforming MOPD by 0.5%,
while preserving general capability at 74.2% and achieving
the best overall average of 64.8%. At the 8B scale, SF-MOPD
reaches 60.4% on the domain-specific average, an improve-
ment of 3.2% over the instruct baseline and 1.4% over vanilla
MOPD. General capability also improves to 76.4%, a gain of
3.1% over MOPD, narrowing the gap with the instruct base-
line at 77.2%. Our SF-MOPD approaches the Expert oracle
reference on most domain-specific benchmarks.
Effect of Capability Reference Design. To isolate the contri-
bution of the reference mechanism, we compare two MOPD
variants on the 8B model. MOPD with Ref adds a KL regu-
larization term between the student and the fixed initialization
model. While this improves general capability from 73.3%
to 73.8% and domain-specific from 59.0% to 59.1% over
vanilla MOPD, the fixed anchor constrains domain-specific
learning, as the student is pulled toward a state without do-
main expertise. SF-MOPD replaces the fixed initialization
with the EMA-smoothed student as a moving reference and
adds the per-teacher slow-fast projection, achieving 60.4% on
domain-specific, a 1.4% gain over vanilla MOPD and 1.3%
over MOPD with Ref, while improving general capability to
76.4%, +3.1% over MOPD and +2.6% over MOPD with Ref.
This confirms that the projection mechanism, rather than a
fixed reference alone, drives the gains.

3.3. Ablation Study
As shown in Table 2, we conduct ablation studies on the
Qwen3-VL-8B model to evaluate our proposed components.
The slow model, i.e., the EMA-ensembled student, serves as
a reference. Removing Slow-Fast Projection (SFP) reduces
the full model to MOPD with an EMA-based reference con-
straint, where the training objective degenerates into directly
pulling the student toward its EMA ensemble, i.e., the classic
Mean-Teacher design. This variant drops the overall average
from 67.7% to 64.0%, a decrease of 3.7%. We then remove
LMOPD and CenterNorm individually:
dropping LMOPD
slightly hurts both general and domain-specific capability,
yielding 75.9% General, 60.2% Domain-Specific, and 67.3%
overall, suggesting that it further preserves the original op-
timization landscape and prevents catastrophic forgetting;
removing CenterNorm also degrades performance to 66.8%
overall compared with 67.7% for the full model, indicating
it stabilizes the projection by aligning distributional statis-
tics. The full SF-MOPD achieves 76.4% General and 60.4%
Domain-Specific, reaching 67.7% overall and outperform-
ing MOPD at 65.5% by 2.2%. Among all components, SFP
yields the largest individual gain with a 3.7% drop when
removed, underscoring the importance of the slow-fast pro-
jection mechanism. We further conduct a sensitivity analysis
on β: setting β to 0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 0.9, and 1.0 yields over-
all averages of 67.7%, 66.1%, 67.3%, 67.2%, and 67.0%,
respectively.
Conflict: Furthermore, we examined whether our method
could yield additional gains when task heterogeneity conflicts
are minimal. By increasing the sampling length of the fine-
grained teacher training data, we reduced response hetero-
geneity across tasks; however, this strategy caused a severe
degradation in fine-grained performance, with ZoomBench
dropping to 40.7%, indicating it is suboptimal for data con-
struction. Comparing vanilla MOPD with SF-MOPD under
these reduced-conflict settings, we found that general-purpose
capability remained nearly unchanged at 76.5% and 77.3%,
respectively. This suggests that even under minimal task het-
erogeneity conflicts, SF-MOPD still preserves general capa-
bilities effectively.
4. CONCLUSION
We have presented SF-MOPD, a slow-fast multi-teacher on-
policy distillation framework that addresses capability inter-
ference, where incorporating new domain expertise gradu-
ally erodes general capabilities. The fast model is the cur-
rent student learning directly from each teacher, while the
slow model, an EMA initialized from the same initialization,
serves as a moving capability reference. The projection re-
moves only the component of each teacher-induced update
that pushes the fast model away from the slow one, leaving
specialized learning uncapped. Extensive experiments across
three model scales demonstrate that SF-MOPD consistently
surpasses the instruct baseline and outperforms prior distilla-
tion methods, achieving the best domain-specific average at
the 2B, 4B and 8B scales and improving the general-domain
trade-off across scales. We hope this work encourages further
research on balancing capability acquisition and preservation
in MLLMs.
