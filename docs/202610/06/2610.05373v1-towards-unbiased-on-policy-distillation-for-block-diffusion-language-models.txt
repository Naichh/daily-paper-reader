## ABSTRACT 

On-policy distillation (OPD) has emerged as an effective post-training paradigm for language models, with recent efforts extending it to block diffusion language models (BDLMs). However, existing studies focus almost exclusively on small block sizes, leaving distillation into student models with larger blocks underex-plored. In this work, we investigate this regime and reveal two critical opti-mization biases that induce severe training instability. First, mismatched block boundaries between teacher and student cause context misalignment , providing distorted supervisory signals that misguide student decoding. Second, even un-der aligned contexts, an intrinsic optimization bias in OPD, where the student tends to rapidly absorb high-support signals while lagging on low-support updates, drives a premature confidence surge that traps weaker students in catastrophic overconfidence collapse. To resolve these, we propose Un-OPD , an unbiased on-policy distillation framework with two novelties for stabilizing BDLM training. First, Un-OPD introduces a boundary-aware step filtering strategy that eliminates context-misaligned decoding steps. Second, Un-OPD proposes moderating opti-mization intensity at high-support positions via a support-rebalanced confidence calibration, thereby bypassing overconfidence collapse. Beyond stability, we also introduce a rollout reuse mechanism to reduce rollout generation overhead. Ex-tensive experiments on math reasoning and code generation benchmarks show that Un-OPD consistently stabilizes training and delivers superior performance while reducing wall-clock training time by approximately half. 

## 1 INTRODUCTION 

Diffusion large language models (dLLMs) have recently emerged as a compelling alternative to autoregressive (AR) models (Achiam et al., 2023; Hui et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2025) by generating multiple tokens in parallel through iterative denoising (Nie et al., 2025; Ye et al., 2025). For example, some open-source models like LLaDA (Nie et al., 2025) and Dream (Ye et al., 2025) maintain a long sequence of mask tokens and iteratively reveal tokens through parallel denoising steps. To inherit the advantages of AR models, particularly KV caching compatibility (Kwon et al., 2023; Ni et al., 2026) and flexible sequence extension, recent dLLM works have advanced toward block diffusion large language models (BDLMs) (Arriola et al., 2025; Cheng et al., 2026; Tian et al., 2025; Wu et al., 2026b; Bie et al., 2025; 2026). By decomposing a sequence into consecutive blocks, BDLMs perform global reasoning autoregressively across blocks while generating tokens via parallel diffusion within each block, achieving a superior quality–latency trade-off. Following the success of post-training in AR models, particularly reinforcement learning with ver-ifiable rewards (RLVR) (Shao et al., 2024; Yu et al., 2026), reinforcement learning (RL) has been actively adapted to enhance the reasoning capabilities of dLLMs (Pan et al., 2026a; Zhao et al., 2026; Wang et al., 2026b; Ou et al., 2026; Ma et al., 2026; Ni et al., 2026). Specifically, to by-pass the intractable conditional likelihood estimation inherent in diffusion denoising, pioneering efforts introduce mean-field surrogates (Zhao et al., 2026; Tang et al., 2026) or tree-structured pol-icy updates (Pan et al., 2026a). Nevertheless, these approaches either rely on coarse outcome-based 1arXiv:2610.05373v1 [cs.CL] 4 Oct 2026 rewards leading to inaccurate and sparse credit assignment across multi-step denoising trajecto-ries (Shao et al., 2026; Zhao et al., 2026), or incur substantial inference overhead to acquire finer-grained reward signals (Pan et al., 2026a). Recently, on-policy distillation (OPD) (Hou et al., 2026; Yang et al., 2026b) has emerged as another promising post-training paradigm. By aligning the stu-dent with teacher predictions along its own rollout trajectories, OPD provides dense, token-level supervision. Recent studies (Hou et al., 2026; Yang et al., 2026b; Fu et al., 2026) demonstrate that OPD outperforms previous RLVR algorithms on language models in terms of both optimization stability and sample efficiency. Despite extensive progress in AR models, OPD for dLLMs remains at an early stage. dOPSD (Dat et al., 2026) extends self-distillation to full-sequence denoising models such as LLaDA (Nie et al., 2025) using predictions from later states with more revealed tokens to supervise earlier states. This design is less applicable to BDLMs, where later states reveal context only within the current block. TOPD (Ren et al., 2026) extends OPD to BDLMs by distilling along the student’s denoising trajec-tories, but it focuses exclusively on small block sizes, leaving distillation into students with larger blocks, which offer inherently greater parallel decoding potential, largely underexplored. In this work, we investigate scaling OPD to BDLMs with larger block sizes. We use small-block BDLMs as teachers because they provide higher-quality generations, and distill them into students with vary-ing block sizes. Our work uncovers two underlying optimization biases that impede the effective distillation of BDLMs: (1) Context misalignment. When denoising within a larger block, the student receives bi-ased supervision because the teacher cannot access its authentic decoding states across mismatched boundaries. Such signals tend to be overly aggressive or excessively conservative, disrupting the student’s confidence-driven decisions. (2) Biased learning dynamics. During OPD, the student prioritizes tokens with strong teacher support over weakly supported ones, causing confidence to prematurely surge and then fall back. This spurious surge drives fragile students into low-quality decoding regimes that are difficult to rectify. To address these two problems, we propose Un-OPD 

(Unbiased On-Policy Distillation), a simple framework for scaling OPD to BDLMs with larger block sizes. Specifically, we first restrict OPD to decoding states with context alignment, ensuring impar-tial teacher supervision signals. We then quantify teacher support to distinguish strongly supported positions and rebalance the teacher support by moderating the update intensity, preventing over-confidence collapse. Beyond stability, we further introduce a rollout reuse mechanism that reuses sampled trajectories for reducing rollout generation overhead. In summary, the main contributions of this work are as follows: • We pioneer the study of OPD on BDLMs with varying block sizes, identifying two underlying optimization bottlenecks: context misalignment arising from mismatched block sizes and biased learning dynamics inherent to standard OPD. • We propose Un-OPD , an unbiased OPD framework that integrates context-aligned step selection and support-rebalanced confidence calibration to stabilize the training dynamics. We also intro-duce a rollout reuse mechanism to reduce rollout overhead during training. • We conduct a comprehensive evaluation across various block sizes and demonstrate that Un-OPD achieves superior performance over counterparts, alongside greater training efficiency. 

## 2 RELATED WORK 

Block Diffusion Language Models. dLLMs (Nie et al., 2025; Zhu et al., 2025; Ye et al., 2025) offer an alternative to autoregressive LLMs (Yang et al., 2025; Achiam et al., 2023) through iterative discrete denoising, with recent gains from scaling data and model size. LLaDA (Nie et al., 2025; Zhu et al., 2025) trains from scratch via masking and unmasking, whereas Dream (Ye et al., 2025) adapts pretrained autoregressive models. Full-sequence dLLMs, however, struggle to combine coherent chain-of-thought reasoning with efficient inference mechanisms such as KV caching (Kwon et al., 2023; Ni et al., 2026). Block diffusion language models (BDLMs) (Arriola et al., 2025; Cheng et al., 2026; Tian et al., 2025; Wu et al., 2026b; Bie et al., 2025; 2026) address this trade-off by generating blocks autoregressively and denoising tokens in parallel within each block. Typically adapted from autoregressive models, BDLMs aim to retain their reasoning capabilities while enabling parallel intra-block generation. 2On-Policy Distillation. By aligning student-generated rollouts with per-token teacher predictions, on-policy distillation (OPD) (Hou et al., 2026; Yang et al., 2026b; Gu et al., 2026; Fu et al., 2026; Xie et al., 2026; Xu et al., 2026; Zhang et al., 2026; Jin et al., 2026; Wang et al., 2026a; Xing et al., 2026) has demonstrated superior post-training efficiency compared to GRPO-style optimiza-tion (Shao et al., 2024; Yu et al., 2026; Gao et al., 2025; Tan et al., 2025) and off-policy coun-terparts (Wang et al., 2023; Ren & Sutherland, 2025; Wu et al., 2026a). To extract more reliable teacher supervision, several works (Zhang et al., 2026; Wang et al., 2026a; Xu et al., 2026; Xie et al., 2026; Zheng et al., 2026) focus optimization on student rollout prefixes. In parallel, on-policy self-distillation (OPSD) (Lu et al., 2026; Yang et al., 2026a; Pan et al., 2026b; Yuan et al., 2026) uses the same model as the teacher, conditioned on privileged context such as ground-truth answers. TOPD (Ren et al., 2026) extends OPD to BDLMs (Cheng et al., 2026) with small block sizes. We investigate instability at larger student block sizes and address context misalignment and biased optimization dynamics. 

Efficient RLVR via Rollout Replay. To improve data efficiency in RLVR, recent works incorpo-rate rollout replay into GRPO-style optimization, curbing policy drift via on/off-policy interleav-ing (Zhang et al., 2025; Zhan et al., 2026; Li et al., 2025), quality filtering (Zhang et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2025), or importance-sampling corrections (Mao et al., 2026). However, these methods require delicate heuristics to manage staleness and off-policy bias. In contrast, we extend rollout replay to OPD, which exhibits fundamentally higher tolerance to trajectory staleness. This allows Un-OPD to employ an aggressive replay schedule that substantially reduces rollout generation overhead without the complexity of trace filtering or data mixing. 

## 3 METHOD 

We propose Un-OPD , a framework that scales OPD to BDLMs with large block sizes by integrating two recipes: context-aligned distillation and support-rebalanced distillation. Our design is driven by two key sources of training instability: context misalignment arising from mismatched block boundaries and biased learning dynamics inherent to standard OPD. In this section, we first provide the preliminaries in Sec. 3.1. Next, Sec. 3.2 analyzes context misalignment and introduces context-aligned distillation. Then, Sec. 3.3 investigates the biased learning dynamics of OPD and introduces our support rebalance strategy. Understanding context misalignment. Unlike autoregressive teacher–student pairs with identical causal contexts, BDLMs with different block sizes can have misaligned visible contexts even under trace-aligned OPD. Fig. 1(a) illustrates this mismatch for teacher and student block sizes of 4 and 8.The student undergoes four commit steps within its 8-token block (yielding states s1–s4), whereas the teacher’s span is split into two 4-token blocks by the red dashed boundary. Consequently, their contexts align only at s4, while s1–s3 suffer from distinct mismatches: at s1, the teacher conditions on the fully generated left-block context that remains noisy to the student, whereas at s2 and s3, the student accesses unmasked context from the right-block that lies strictly beyond the teacher’s first block boundary. 

Optimization bias under context misalignment. To probe how context misalignment affects stu-dent optimization, we pair an SDAR-4B-b8 student with a TraDo-8B-b4 teacher and categorize decoding states into three regimes: G1 (student sees more context such as s2, s 3), G2 (teacher sees more such as s1), and G3 (contexts are aligned such as s4). Let hi = arg max v pS,i (v) denote the student’s top-1 token at position i. We quantify the teacher support ρi = pT,i (hi)/p S,i (hi),where ρi > 1 and ρi < 1 denote stronger and weaker teacher support for the student’s top-1 pre-diction, respectively. In Fig. 1(b), the blue box plots show the teacher support distributions across the three state groups, while the orange box plots show the distributions of changes in the student’s top-1 probability after training with OPD exclusively on each group. The teacher support skews predominantly below 1.0 in G1 and above 1.0 in G2, whereas G3 remains well-balanced. The confi-dence change mirrors this pattern: G1 yields negligible confidence change, G2 causes a sharp surge, and G3 produces a moderate rise. These results demonstrate that context asymmetry systematically biases teacher supervision signals on the student rollout tokens in two opposite directions: • Under-supportive supervision signals. With less visible context than the student, the teacher tends to underestimate the student’s valid rollouts relative to aligned contexts, which leaves the student at risk of failing to reinforce confidence in correct predictions through OPD. • Over-supportive supervision signals. With more visible context than the student, the teacher tends to provide overly aggressive support relative to aligned contexts, which puts the student at risk of becoming overconfident in incorrect predictions through OPD.

Though we have identified and mitigated context-induced bias in Sec. 3.2, training remains sub-optimal. Crucially, we observe that another bias, the internal optimization dynamics of OPD, also compromises BDLM training stability. In this part, we first present empirical observations of this behavior in OPD, then provide a theoretical grounding, and finally introduce a simple yet effective solution. 

Biased optimization dynamics in OPD. Since top-1 token confidence critically dictates decoding decisions in BDLMs, we begin by examining the evolution of the student’s average top-1 confidence throughout the entire OPD training process. As illustrated in Fig. 2, decoding confidence of the student model exhibits a distinct two-stage pattern throughout training: a rapid surge during the early phase, followed by a slight decline during later training. To dissect the underlying mechanism behind this phenomenon, we leverage the previously defined teacher support ρi in Sec. 3.2 given its tight correlation with confidence shifts, and divide the supervised positions into two distinct groups: 

I+ = {i : ρi > 1} and I− = {i : ρi < 1}. Empirically, updates from I+ tend to increase the student's decoding confidence, whereas updates from I− tend to decrease it.

supervisory signals from high-support po-sitions ( I+), whereas updates from low-support positions ( I−) suffer from an op-timization lag . Rollout reuse. We notice that OPD is inherently better suited for trajectory reuse compared to RLVR. In OPD, historical rollouts serve merely as conditioning states rather than frozen discrete actions or stale scalar rewards as in RLVR. Across multiple optimization steps on the same rollout batch, we recompute the student distributions under the current policy, while the frozen teacher distributions over the fixed states remain unchanged. The calibrated targets and distillation gradients are recomputed accordingly. Consequently, the primary discrepancy in OPD replay arises solely from state distribution mismatch between the sampling policy and the updated student. Unlike RLVR, which suffers from volatile importance sampling and reward clipping over historical rollouts, OPD suffers from substantially milder off-policy degradation. This insight motivates our rollout reuse mechanism, which performs multiple updates per rollout to effectively reduce the heavy cost of rollout sampling.

## 5 CONCLUSION 

In this work, we pioneer the study of scaling on-policy distillation (OPD) to block diffusion language models (BDLMs) with varying block sizes. We uncover two fundamental optimization bottlenecks: 

context misalignment induced by mismatched block boundaries, and overconfidence collapse trig-gered by biased learning dynamics under reverse-KL supervision. To overcome these challenges, we introduce Un-OPD , which eliminates supervisory distortions via boundary-aware step filtering and 9recalibrates optimization intensity across token supports. In addition, we integrate a rollout reuse mechanism that roughly halves training overhead with negligible accuracy loss. Comprehensive experiments on mathematical reasoning and code generation demonstrate that Un-OPD achieves re-markable stability and superior accuracy as well as substantial efficiency. Despite these gains, the effectiveness of Un-OPD may depend on the quality and suitability of the teacher’s supervision. Fur-thermore, pushing this Pareto frontier toward ultra-high parallelism regimes presents a compelling avenue for future research.
