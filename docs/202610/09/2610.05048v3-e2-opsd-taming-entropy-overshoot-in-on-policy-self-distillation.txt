# ABSTRACT 

On-policy self-distillation (OPSD) provides dense token-level supervision with-out a second model: one network acts as teacher with the reference solution and as student with only the problem. We identify a specific failure mode of this recipe. During training, student token entropy rises past the teacher’s and remains elevated, a pattern we call entropy overshoot . We trace it to both sides of distil-lation. The reference-conditioned teacher is confident along its answer-directed reasoning path, but this confidence transfers poorly to student-generated prefixes, making its supervision overly tied to answer-specific cues rather than reusable reasoning patterns; meanwhile, the forward KL used by OPSD continually dif-fuses the student’s predictive distribution without pulling it back. We introduce 

E2-OPSD to address both causes. Exemplar-guided teaching replaces the cur-rent answer with a retrieved solved neighboring problem, providing transferable reasoning guidance without revealing the destination and better matching student-reachable states. Entropy-aware distillation uses the student–teacher entropy gap to determine the direction and strength of each token’s correction. E2-OPSD 

improves math reasoning by up to 4.3 points in mean@16 over OPSD, while out-of-domain evaluations show gains over the corresponding base models of up to 4.9 points in mean@16 and 5.5 points in pass@8. Despite these gains, E2-OPSD 

remains simple, requiring no additional forward passes or networks.

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) is now the standard recipe for improv-ing LLM reasoning, yet its supervision remains coarse: an entire rollout collapses into a sin-gle scalar reward (Shao et al., 2024; Guo et al., 2025). On-policy distillation (OPD) instead of-fers dense, token-level supervision by having a teacher score the student’s own rollouts (Agarwal et al., 2024; Gu et al., 2023), and is now a standard post-training component in Qwen3 (Yang et al., 2025), GLM-5 (GLM-5-Team et al., 2026), and DeepSeek-V4 (DeepSeek-AI et al., 2026). De-spite this fine granularity, it still requires a stronger open-weight teacher that shares the student’s vo-cabulary. On-policy self-distillation (OPSD) further removes the need to serve a second model: a copy of the student acts as the teacher, with access to the reference solution, while the student sees only the problem. Teacher and student are evaluated on the same student-generated prefixes and matched with forward KL (Zhao et al., 2026). This makes OPSD an appealing way to turn privi-leged solution information into dense supervision. In practice, however, its gains are unstable and can deteriorate as training proceeds.

Standard OPSD gives the teacher the current reference solution, letting it jump from the student prefix toward the answer. E2-OPSD instead uses a solved neighbor as a strategy guide, forcing the teacher to follow the student’s intermediate step and solve the current problem itself, turning answer leakage into strategy transfer. Entropy-aware distillation switches between them. We identify a characteristic failure mode, as shown in Figure 1 (left), entropy overshoot : instead of settling toward the teacher, the student becomes progressively more uncertain, eventually surpass-ing the teacher’s entropy and staying there. Entropy overshoot therefore describes a regime where useful exploration spills into persistent and excessive uncertainty, gradually eroding the student’s decisiveness and amplifying teacher-side uncertainty into noisy student behavior. We examine this failure from both teacher and student perspectives. First, OPSD produces supervi-sion mismatched to the student’s reasoning path . The teacher is confident on its own trajectories but becomes substantially more uncertain on student-generated prefixes (Figure 1, right), suggesting that its guidance does not transfer cleanly to the intermediate states the student actually reaches. This mismatch stems from a deeper issue: seeing the current solution lets the teacher narrate toward a known answer rather than reason from scratch, making its supervision answer-specific rather than transferable (Figure 2(a)). Second, OPSD lacks a mechanism to regulate policy spread . Forward KL is beneficial when the student is too concentrated as it expands probability toward teacher-supported alternatives. But the same pressure persists even after the policy becomes overly diffuse, which makes exploration a one-way ratchet: uncertainty keeps spreading, with no force pulling the policy back into focus (Figure 2(b)). These observations motivate E2-OPSD , a simple framework that repairs both sides of the learning process with Exemplar-guided teaching and Entropy-aware distillation, as illustrated in Figure 3. More specifically, E2-OPSD makes three contributions: • What to learn — Exemplar-guided teaching. We replace the current problem’s reference solution in the teacher’s context with a retrieved solved neighboring problem. The exemplar provides a transferable problem-solving reference without revealing the current answer. Conditioned on this reference and the student’s generated prefix, the teacher’s token-level guidance is meant to be more compatible with the student’s ongoing reasoning. • How to learn — Entropy-aware distillation. We use the student–teacher entropy gap as a feed-back signal: when the student is more concentrated than the teacher, forward KL encourages exploration; when it becomes more diffuse, reverse KL suppresses unsupported mass and encour-ages concentration. The gap magnitude downweights uncertain routing decisions and concentrates learning on tokens with larger entropy mismatches. This turns self-distillation from a fixed one-way objective into closed-loop self-balancing control of exploration. • Simple and Generalized. E 2-OPSD adds no extra forward passes during distillation and mini-mum overhead. Across various model families, sizes from 0.6B to 7B, and both thinking and instruction-tuned models, it mitigates persistent entropy overshoot and improves math reason-ing by up to 4.3 points over OPSD, with gains transferring to out-of-domain tasks by up to 4.9 mean@16 points over the corresponding base model. These results suggest that effective self-2Figure 3: Overview of E2-OPSD .

distillation depends not on maximizing imitation, but on aligning teacher guidance with the student’s reasoning path and regulating when the student should explore versus concentrate .

On-policy self-distillation. On-policy self-distillation (OPSD) instantiates Eq. 2 with a copy of the student as teacher, πT = πθ0 , whose context includes the reference solution y∗ unseen by the student and is matched via forward KL (Zhao et al., 2026). This privileged teacher creates an irreducible objective gap (Yang et al., 2026), overweights stylistic and answer-implied tokens over genuine decisions (Pan et al., 2026; Harne et al., 2026), and suppresses uncertainty important to thinking models (Kaur et al., 2026; Kim et al., 2026). Existing remedies largely patch these effects downstream. Verifier-directed methods outsource update control to outcome rewards (Yang et al., 2026; Ke et al., 2026; He et al., 2026), effectively reintroducing RLVR. Signal-purification methods cancel privileged bias with an additional teacher pass (Pan et al., 2026; Shen et al., 2026), paying extra compute after the bias is already introduced. Context-curation methods expose less of the solution or replace it with a successful sibling rollout (Han et al., 2026; H¨ ubotter et al., 2026), but still rely on solution-specific supervision and auxiliary control. These approaches mitigate symptoms without removing their source: teacher guidance tied to the current problem’s solution. E2-OPSD 

removes this mismatch at the source, restores the concentrating correction absent from forward KL, and retains a single frozen-teacher pass with no reward. 

Entropy-aware post-training. Entropy has become a common control signal in post-training, but serves different roles across RLVR and distillation. In RLVR, it is primarily used to regulate exploration, from preventing entropy collapse to concentrating updates on high-entropy or decision-critical tokens (Cui et al., 2025; Yu et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2025; Hao et al., 2025). In distillation, entropy has been used to guide supervision selection and adaptation: EOPD activates additional forward-KL supervision at high-teacher-entropy tokens, while subsequent work further adapts su-pervision across uncertainty regimes (Jin et al., 2026; Zhang et al., 2026; Lee et al., 2026). E2-OPSD 

instead targets the relative entropy dynamics of the student and teacher, using their signed token-level entropy gap to correct entropy overshoot during on-policy self-distillation. 

Training-time contextual guidance. A broad line of work improves post-training by providing auxiliary context that is available during training but absent at inference. Context distillation inter-nalizes prompts, exemplars, or scratchpads into model weights (Askell et al., 2021; Snell et al., 2022; Shin et al., 2025; Kalwar et al., 2026). More recent methods diversify the form of this guidance: OPCD distills experiential knowledge from historical solution traces, SDFT learns on-policy from demonstrations, SKALD conditions a self-teacher on abstract skill cards, and PMD accumulates reusable procedural memories across training episodes (Ye et al., 2026; Shenfeld et al., 2026; Jiang et al., 2026; Liu et al., 2026). Other approaches inject more instance-specific scaffolds directly into optimization, such as partial solutions in QuestA, off-policy reasoning traces in LUFFY, in-context exemplars in XRPO, or retrieved analogous demonstrations in RA-RFT (Li et al., 2026a; Yan et al., 2025; Bamba et al., 2025; Xiao et al., 2026). OPSD conditions its self-teacher on the current prob-lem’s reference solution; E2-OPSD instead uses a retrieved solved neighbor, transferring reusable strategy across instances without revealing the current answer. 

# 3 METHOD 

E2-OPSD repairs both sides of the entropy-overshoot failure identified in Section 1: exemplar-guided teaching changes what the teacher is conditioned on so its guidance is grounded in transfer-able reasoning rather than answer-specific cues, while entropy-aware distillation changes how the student matches it, using the per-token entropy gap to set both the direction and strength of the cor-rection. Both run inside the standard OPSD loop with one frozen teacher and no additional forward passes or extra network.

EGT improves what the teacher provides; Entropy-Aware Distillation (EAD) controls how the stu-dent follows it. Building on the complementary mode-covering and mode-seeking biases of forward and reverse KL (Section 2.1), EAD has two components: Bidirectional Entropy Routing (BER) se-lects the KL direction from the student–teacher entropy gap, while Adaptive Gap Damping (AGD) uses its magnitude to scale the update. 

Bidirectional entropy routing. We use the entropy gap ∆t = H(pt) − H(qt) to determine which correction the student needs. When ∆t ≤ 0, the student is relatively more concentrated than the teacher, so BER applies forward KL to broaden coverage. When ∆t > 0, the student is relatively more diffuse, so BER switches to reverse KL to refocus the policy (Figure 2(b)). # 4 EXPERIMENTS 

4.1 EXPERIMENTAL SETTINGS 

Models & Datasets. We evaluate four configurations across the Qwen3 (Yang et al., 2025) and Olmo3 (Olmo et al., 2026) families: Qwen3-0.6B and Qwen3-1.7B with thinking enabled, Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507, and Olmo-3-7B-Instruct. The reasoning mode is consistent across the student, teacher (where applicable), and evaluation. Following Pan et al. (2026), we use DeepMath-103K (He et al., 2025) for training, with a difficulty band assigned to each configuration (Appendix A.1). We evaluate in-domain mathematical reasoning on AMC23 (math-ai, 2023), AIME’24 (Zhang & Math-AI, 2024), AIME’25 (Zhang & Math-AI, 2025), and HMMT’25 Feb (Harvard–MIT Mathe-matics Tournament, 2025). Out-of-domain evaluation covers logical reasoning on a fixed, randomly sampled 100-puzzle subset of ZebraLogic (Lin et al., 2025)for computational tractability, and code generation on HumanEval (Chen et al., 2021). 

Baselines. We compare against (1) Base , the untrained checkpoints; (2) OPSD (Zhao et al., 2026), which distills a frozen teacher conditioned on the current problem’s reference solution using forward KL; (3) SDPO (H¨ ubotter et al., 2026), which uses an EMA teacher conditioned on a successful sib-ling rollout and a generalized JSD loss; (4) GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), which optimizes a clipped policy objective using group-normalized advantages from verifiable rewards, with KL regularization against a frozen reference policy; (5) RLSD (Yang et al., 2026), which uses verifiable rewards to determine update directions and self-distillation signals to modulate token-level update magnitudes; and (6) OPSD + EOPD , our self-distillation adaptation of EOPD (Jin et al., 2026), which combines reverse KL with forward KL at high-teacher-entropy positions. We retain this entropy-gated diver-gence but replace EOPD’s external stronger teacher with OPSD’s reference-conditioned self-teacher for a controlled comparison. 

Implementation Details. We use veRL (Sheng et al., 2025) with vLLM (Kwon et al., 2023) on 8×H200 GPUs and a learning rate of 10 −6. We faithfully implement each baseline following the algorithmic design in its original paper, preserving its method-specific objectives and update rules, including teacher construction for self-distillation methods. All trained methods and ablations share the same training settings and a budget of 360 optimizer steps. Detailed baseline configurations, including the explicit adaptation of EOPD to the OPSD setting, are provided in Appendix A.4 for reproducibility. The baseline results in Table 1 are obtained from these implementations under the shared protocol. For self-distillation methods, the teacher is frozen at its initial weights except in SDPO and RLSD. We use K = 15 for a diverse exemplar selection, and supply retrieved exemplars only to the teacher. We evaluate each model with three evaluation seeds to reduce noise. We provide the model with only the original questions during evaluation inference. See Appendix A for detailed training and evaluation settings. 4.2 MAIN RESULTS

E2-OPSD mitigates persistent entropy overshoot. Figures 4(a) and 4(b) revisit the failure iden-tified in the introduction. Under OPSD, student entropy crosses the teacher early and continues to drift upward, creating a persistent entropy gap. In contrast, E2-OPSD keeps student entropy tightly regulated around the teacher throughout training, preventing runaway diffusion while preserving sufficient exploration. This curbs entropy overshoot and stabilizes distillation. 

E2-OPSD exhibits an emergent exploration-to-concentration transition. As shown in Fig-ure 4(c), forward KL dominates early, promoting exploration toward teacher-supported alternatives, while reverse KL becomes increasingly prevalent later, suppressing excess probability mass and concentrating the student distribution. This shift emerges without an explicit schedule and natu-rally tracks the evolving student state. The trajectories reveal distinct distributional dynamics under fixed versus state-dependent KL routing. 

State-dependent KL routing outperforms fixed directions. With the reference-answer teacher fixed and adaptive gap damping disabled, our entropy-aware routing clearly outperforms all other methods that apply fixed KL directions (see Figure 6). To be more precise, forward KL keeps expanding the student and eventually drives entropy overshoot, making the student overly diffuse. Reverse KL suppresses excess mass but also restricts exploration when the student has not yet cov-ered enough plausible alternatives. Their fixed mixture softens both extremes, yet applies expansion and contraction simultaneously regardless of the student’s current state, causing the two corrections to partially counteract each other rather than selecting the one that is needed. In contrast, E2-OPSD 

uses the student–teacher entropy gap to select the needed correction, expanding when the student is too concentrated and contracting when it is too diffuse, yielding more stable entropy dynamics and stronger validation performance. 

Exemplar guidance makes the teacher locally learnable. Figure 5(b) reveals why replacing the current reference solution matters. When conditioned on the reference answer, the teacher produces much shorter and more confident trajectories than the student, with an entropy of 0.190 nats versus the student’s 0.268 nats. EGT instead keeps the teacher closer to the student’s reasoning regime, at 2,090 tokens and 0.261 nats. Moreover, EGT reduces teacher–student forward KL by 25.9% (0.1549 to 0.1148 nats) on the same student prefixes. Together, these observations suggest that EGT brings the teacher’s native uncertainty closer to the student’s reasoning regime while providing more closely aligned token-level guidance on student-visited states. 

AGD sharpens entropy-aware routing. Figure 5(c) isolates how AGD redistributes token-level learning signals. Here, high entropy means H ≥ ln 2 , corresponding to at least two effective next-token choices. Entropy-discordant positions—HL and LH, where student and teacher fall into opposite high/low entropy regimes—account for only 7.67% of all response tokens, yet con-tribute 20.18% of the relative gradient mass without AGD. Enabling AGD further raises this share to 28.50%, with both HL and LH increasing consistently. This shows that AGD does not uniformly rescale the distillation objective; instead, it makes BER more selective by shifting learning toward positions with clearer student–teacher disagreement in policy spread. To rule out that small entropy gaps merely hide distributional mismatch, we measure probability-mass overlap: strongly damped positions have 0.985 mean overlap (Appendix C), supporting the entropy gap as a simple yet prin-cipled proxy for local distributional mismatch. In this sense, BER determines which direction to correct, while AGD determines which routing decisions deserve stronger updates .

# 5 CONCLUSION 

We identify entropy overshoot as a key failure mode of on-policy self-distillation: a teacher that has read the answer supplies guidance the student cannot fully learn, and OPSD’s forward-KL ob-jective keeps pushing the student outward, turning useful exploration into persistent distributional 9mismatch. We introduce E2-OPSD , which controls this process by constructing more locally learn-able teacher targets and adapting both the direction and the strength of distillation to the student’s training state. Across both thinking and instruct models, E2-OPSD consistently improves model’s reasoning capability, with gains transferring to out-of-domain tasks. Our analyses show that effec-tive self-distillation requires matching teacher guidance to student-reachable states and uncertainty structure, rather than simply increasing or suppressing entropy. These results suggest that control-ling where exploration occurs is as important as encouraging exploration itself.

p(S), q (S) ≥ 1 − η for 0 ≤ η ≤ 1/2. For r ∈ { p, q }, define its restriction to S by rS (v) =

∆ = H(p) − H(q), ∆S = H(pS ) − H(qS ).

r within S. Thus, when |∆| > B η , a negative entropy gap indicates fewer effective alternatives under p than under q within the shared set, while a positive gap indicates more. For a fixed finite vocabulary, the error bound vanishes as η → 0.
