## ABSTRACT 

Multimodal on-policy distillation (OPD) aims to improve visual grounding while preserving the strong reasoning capabilities of language models. Recent multi-teacher approaches combine LLM and VLM teachers to provide complementary supervision. However, directly using a VLM’s full predictive distribution entan-gles its visual grounding signal with its own language prior, preventing the ground-ing information from being transferred independently. Conversely, increasing the strength of visual supervision can improve perception but may overemphasize vi-sual evidence and degrade language reasoning. To address this trade-off, we intro-duce LEGO-OPD , which selectively composes factors from a Language Expert and a Grounding expert into One teacher distribution for multimodal OPD. Under a generalized Bayesian formulation, the language expert provides a prior over can-didate tokens, while the grounding expert contributes a visual likelihood that up-dates this prior, rather than transferring its complete predictive distribution. This factorized composition allows language reasoning and visual grounding to be con-trolled independently. We further introduce adaptive calibration to determine how strongly the visual likelihood should update the language prior at each decod-ing prefix. Specifically, LEGO-OPD uses the grounding expert’s image-induced prediction shift as a prefix-dependent reference, preventing both insufficient and excessive visual supervision. Experiments with Qwen3 models show that LEGO-OPD consistently outperforms the evaluated single- and multi-teacher OPD base-lines on both multimodal and text-only reasoning tasks. Moreover, it improves the initial student’s visual perception while preserving text-only reasoning. 

## 1 INTRODUCTION 

On-policy distillation (OPD) is an effective post-training framework for multimodal reasoning in vision-language models (VLMs), distilling teacher predictions at student-generated prefixes (Agar-wal et al., 2024; Yin et al., 2026). Effective multimodal OPD requires reliable visual grounding and strong reasoning over grounded information. Recent approaches largely follow two directions. The first introduces additional specialized teachers to provide complementary supervision, for example by combining external large language models (LLMs) for reasoning with VLM teachers for multimodal guidance (Kwai Keye Team et al., 2026; Yin et al., 2026). However, directly combining their complete predictive distributions entangles each teacher’s language prior and grounding signal, making it difficult to selectively transfer their complementary strengths. The second direction strengthens the visual component of the distillation signal to improve grounding (Liu et al., 2026; Liang et al., 2026; Yoon et al., 2026). While stronger visual supervision can improve perception, excessive emphasis on visual evidence may distort the underlying language prior and degrade reasoning performance. Together, these limitations high-light the need for a distillation framework that can separately control language reasoning and visual grounding while combining them into a coherent teacher distribution. In this paper, we introduce LEGO-OPD, which constructs a single teacher distribution by composing complementary factors from independently selected experts. Our method consists of two compo-nents. First, we factorize the teacher into a language prior and a visual grounding factor. This composition admits a generalized Bayesian interpretation in which the language distribution acts as a prior and the visual signal acts as a likelihood that updates the prior into a unified teacher distribution. Second, we introduce source-referenced calibration to determine how strongly the visual factor should modify the language prior at each prefix. Existing prediction-space composition methods, such as DExperts, proxy-tuning, and visual contrastive decoding, typically rely on fixed composition coefficients (Liu et al., 2021; 2024; Leng et al., 2024). However, a fixed coefficient can induce substantially different prediction shifts depending on the underlying prior and decoding prefix. We therefore use the grounding expert’s source KL , which measures the change in its prediction induced by the image, as a prefix-dependent reference for calibrating the visual update. The scaling coefficient is adapted so that the magnitude of the visual update is normalized relative to this reference, thereby balancing visual grounding against language-prior supervision. A global multiplier on the reference further controls the desired degree of visual emphasis. The composed teacher supervises the student through standard reverse-KL on-policy distillation. Experiments with Qwen3 models show that LEGO-OPD consistently outperforms the evaluated OPD baselines on both multimodal and text-only reasoning tasks. Further analysis shows that adap-tive calibration strengthens visual grounding while preserving text-only reasoning ability. Ablation studies confirm that both factorized teacher composition and adaptive calibration are essential to achieving these improvements. Our contributions are summarized as follows. • Factorized teacher composition. We compose a language prior and a visual likelihood estimate from independently selected experts into a single OPD teacher through generalized Bayesian updating. • Source-referenced calibration. We adaptively determine the visual update’s strength at each prefix, with a global scale controlling the overall visual emphasis. • Empirical evaluation. We demonstrate improvements over the evaluated OPD baselines in both multimodal and text-only reasoning, and show that adaptive calibration enables stronger visual perception without sacrificing text-only reasoning performance. 

## 2 RELATED WORKS AND BACKGROUND 

2.1 RELATED WORK 

Heterogeneous Teachers for Multimodal Distillation. Recent OPD methods use specialized teachers with complementary strengths, an approach also explored in multimodal distillation. These methods differ in the granularity of teacher selection and aggregation. For example, M-OPD routes 2Preprint. examples to domain-specific teachers (Ma et al., 2026), and Keye-VL extends routing across modal-ities and tasks (Kwai Keye Team et al., 2026). ViGOS uses differently conditioned self-teachers for perception and reasoning segments (Wang et al., 2026), while H-OPD combines LLM and VLM predictions by confidence at each token (Yin et al., 2026). However, external LLM supervision can require additional text-only training data or image descriptions (Appendix C.5). Routing teachers or mixing their complete predictions still entangles the multimodal teacher’s language prior and visual grounding signal, limiting independent transfer of each expert’s strengths. 

Visual Supervision in Multimodal Distillation. A complementary line of multimodal OPD work strengthens the visual information carried by the supervision signal. VA-OPD uses sensitivity to vi-sual detail to reweight rollouts and balance supervision across token groups (Liu et al., 2026). VCSD refines teacher targets through original-image and content-erased contrasts, but reports performance degradation under excessive visual emphasis (Liang et al., 2026). Decomposed OPD combines an auxiliary visual objective with a language preservation loss to mitigate interference from excessive visual emphasis (Yoon et al., 2026). These findings highlight the importance of balancing visual and language-prior supervision. 

Composition in Prediction Space. Prediction-space composition offers a way to transfer signals across models. DExperts uses expert and anti-expert predictions (Liu et al., 2021), proxy-tuning transfers prediction shifts from smaller tuned models (Liu et al., 2024), and UniR adds learned reasoning-module logits to a frozen LLM (Kim et al., 2026). Visual contrastive decoding simi-larly amplifies prediction differences across image conditions (Leng et al., 2024). However, these methods use fixed composition coefficients, leaving the choice of suitable composition strengths to predefined settings or empirical tuning. LEGO-OPD composes complementary expert factors instead of complete predictions. Source-referenced calibration selects composition strength by adapting the visual update at each prefix. 2.2 GENERALIZED BAYESIAN UPDATING

## 4 EXPERIMENTS 

4.1 EXPERIMENTAL SETUP 

Models and training data. For LEGO-OPD, we use Qwen3-VL-2B-Instruct as the student, Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 as the language expert, and Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct as the grounding expert (Yang et al., 2025; Bai et al., 2025). We use the 54,840-example subset of MMFineReason-123K released by H-OPD as the multimodal training data (Lin et al., 2026; Yin et al., 2026). This subset supports comparison with H-OPD and includes the image descriptions required by its language teacher. 

Training. All experiments, including ablations, use a common training implementation, student initialization, and optimization settings, except for method-specific components. All student param-6Preprint. eters are updated for 200 steps using AdamW (Loshchilov & Hutter, 2019) with learning rate 10 −6,no warmup, and global batch size 128. Prompt and response lengths are limited to 12,384 tokens. Rollouts use temperature 1.0 without top-p or top-k truncation. We follow the top-16 OPD setting of Li et al. (2026). Implementation details are in Appendices B and C.1. 

Evaluation. We evaluate multimodal reasoning, text-only reasoning, and visual perception sepa-rately, as summarized in Table 1. The multimodal reasoning benchmarks comprise MATH-Vision (Wang et al., 2024a), MathVerse (Zhang et al., 2025), MMMU-Pro (Yue et al., 2025), MathVista (Lu et al., 2024), RBench-M (Guo et al., 2025), and OlympiadBench-MM (He et al., 2024). For text-only evaluation, we use MATH-500 (Lightman et al., 2024), MathVerse-Text Only (Zhang et al., 2025), MMLU-Pro (Wang et al., 2024b), RBench-T (Guo et al., 2025), OlympiadBench-Text (He et al., 2024). To assess visual perception separately from reasoning performance, we evaluate BLINK (Fu et al., 2025) and QBench1,2 (Wu et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2024). For QBench1,2, we pool the perception-focused Group A questions from both benchmarks (1,495 + 1,000 questions) and report accuracy over the combined set. Except for perception, we average five samples per question. Cate-gory averages are unweighted benchmark means. Evaluation details are provided in Appendix C.3. 

Baselines. We compare LEGO-OPD with standard OPD, VA-OPD (Liu et al., 2026), M-OPD (Ma et al., 2026), and H-OPD (Yin et al., 2026). For controlled comparison, we match the model scale, optimization settings, and response budget whenever compatible with each method, while preserving its defining supervision structure. M-OPD and H-OPD use both VLM and LLM teachers, whereas standard OPD and VA-OPD rely only on a VLM teacher. Since M-OPD is formulated for domain-routed text distillation, we route our multimodal training data to the VLM teacher and DeepMath to the LLM teacher. VA-OPD retains its multi-response sampling scheme, generating four responses per prompt and therefore observing fewer unique training prompts under the same response budget. ## 5 CONCLUSION 

We presented LEGO-OPD , which composes a language prior and a visual grounding signal into a unified teacher for multimodal on-policy distillation. A generalized Bayesian formulation en-ables factorized teacher composition, while source-referenced calibration adaptively controls the visual contribution at each student-generated prefix. A local KL approximation yields a closed-form calibration coefficient, with its approximation error assessed against a numerical KL-matching reference. Across Qwen3 models, LEGO-OPD achieves the best average performance among the evaluated single- and multi-teacher OPD baselines on both multimodal and text-only reasoning. Ab-lations show that the language prior and visual residual provide complementary benefits, and that adaptive calibration outperforms fixed weighting in both reasoning averages while allowing stronger visual emphasis without degrading reasoning. Overall, LEGO-OPD provides an effective framework for balancing language reasoning and visual grounding in multimodal distillation. Limitations are discussed in Appendix F. 9Preprint.

We use the LEGO-OPD training setup in Table 5 whenever compatible with each baseline. Unless otherwise noted, we retain the objectives and method-specific hyperparameters reported in the orig-inal papers, while adapting the model configuration, data, and training budget to our experimental setting. 

Standard OPD. Standard OPD distills directly from a frozen Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct teacher con-ditioned on the image and question. We use the same MMFineReason data, rollout configuration, and student-top-16 OPD surrogate as LEGO-OPD. 

VA-OPD. VA-OPD retains its multi-response training scheme, generating four responses per prompt and therefore using 32 unique prompts per update under the common 128-response budget. Follow-ing the reported configuration, the VLM teacher scores each response with the original and pixelated image to estimate visual importance. We use a degradation ratio of 0.1, assign the highest-advantage 20% of token positions to the high-importance group, and give equal weight to the high- and low-importance groups. Training uses full-vocabulary reverse KL rather than the student-top-16 surro-gate. 

M-OPD. M-OPD was originally formulated for domain-routed multi-teacher distillation over text domains. We adapt it to the multimodal setting by routing examples from our multimodal training data (the 54,840-example MMFineReason subset in Section 4.1) to the VLM teacher and DeepMath-103K (He et al., 2026) examples to the LLM teacher at a 1:1 ratio. Following M-OPD’s policy-gradient formulation, we use sampled-token distillation with the reported advantage clipping mag-nitude of 5 instead of a top-K objective. 

H-OPD. H-OPD combines a VLM teacher conditioned on the image and question with an LLM teacher conditioned on the released description-augmented input. Following its reported configura-tion, each teacher distribution is normalized over its top-eight tokens, and the two distributions are combined using entropy-based confidence weights with temperature 0.5. The student is trained with reverse KL over the union of the two teacher supports. This support contains at most 16 tokens and is distinct from the student-top-16 support used by Standard OPD and LEGO-OPD.

Expert-factor substitution. To examine the contributions of the two factor sources, we replace either the grounding expert or the language prior with the corresponding factor from the frozen initial student, as described in Section 4.3. Grounding-expert substitution uses the frozen student’s image-conditioned and image-free predictions to construct the visual residual. Language-prior substitution instead uses the frozen student’s image-free prediction as the receiving prior. The remaining expert is retained, and the calibration statistics are computed from the resulting factor sources.

## F LIMITATIONS 

Although LEGO-OPD calibrates how strongly visual evidence modifies the language prior, it does not determine whether that evidence is reliable. Source KL measures the magnitude of the grounding expert’s visual update rather than its correctness, so calibration can amplify erroneous evidence. Beyond calibration, the visual residual represents a conditional model contrast rather than an iso-lated visual factor, because generated prefixes may already contain information inferred from the image. In addition, the current implementation requires a shared tokenization and output vocabu-lary, restricting composition to compatible models within the same family. 33
