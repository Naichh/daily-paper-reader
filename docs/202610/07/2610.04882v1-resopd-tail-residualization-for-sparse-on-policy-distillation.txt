On-policy distillation (OPD) trains a student model on self-generated trajectories, but transmitting dense teacher distributions across long reasoning traces creates prohibitive communication and memory bottlenecks. Practical systems therefore rely on sparse teacher interfaces, typically transmitting either the sampled-token score or a small Top-𝑘 distribution. However, this sparse setting faces a fundamental dilemma: sampled-token estimators are unbiased but suffer from severe gradient variance, whereas directly optimizing Top-𝑘 objectives introduces systematic bias. To improve this trade-off, we propose ResOPD ( On-Policy Distillation with Tail 

Res idualization), which provides unbiased full-vocabulary reverse KL gradient estimation under on-policy sampling, with substantial variance reduction under the same sparse payload in the evaluated settings. ResOPD aggregates the unobserved vocabulary into an observable coarse tail event, computes its exact aggregate gradient, and samples only the fine-grained within-tail residual, which requires no additional teacher queries or forward passes. Extensive experiments demonstrate that ResOPD substantially reduces gradient variance, stabilizes online training dynamics, and improves downstream performance across the evaluated settings. These results establish ResOPD as an efficient, plug-and-play variance reduction primitive for sparse on-policy distillation. 

Keywords: Large Language Models, On-Policy Distillation, Variance Reduction, Sparse Teacher Feedback 

# 1. Introduction 

On-policy distillation (OPD) [1, 9] transfers reasoning and generation ability from a strong teacher to a smaller student. Unlike offline distillation [17] or supervised fine-tuning, it trains the student on self-generated trajectories with token-level teacher supervision, which reduces exposure bias over long sequences. OPD is now a standard step in Large Language Model (LLM) post-training at frontier labs, including Thinking Machines [20], GLM [29], and DeepSeek [27]. Standard OPD [1] needs the teacher’s full distribution at every generated token, which is prohibitively costly for modern LLMs whose vocabularies can reach 250K tokens, so the payload grows with vocabulary size per token and with rollout length per sequence. Practical training systems therefore expose a sparse teacher interface that, at each step, returns only the teacher’s Top-𝑘 tokens with their probabilities, along with the probability of the sampled token [12, 30]. We refer to OPD trained through such an interface as sparse OPD . The difficulty is that the omitted tail is never observed: objectives defined directly on the Top-𝑘 support, whether by truncation or renormalization [32] or by aggregating the omitted mass into a ghost token [3, 12], are surrogates of the full-vocabulary reverse Kullback-Leibler (rKL) objective and carry a systematic gradient bias (Fig. 1(b)). Bias can be traded for variance: rather than shrinking the objective to the observed support, a second family of methods retains the full-vocabulary rKL objective and estimates its gradient from the sparse payload, remaining unbiased but noisy . However, existing estimators fail to fully exploit this payload. At one extreme, sampled-token OPD [9, 20] queries only the rollout token’s score, suffering from severe gradient variance due to single-sample Monte Carlo estimation (Fig. 1(b)). Consequently, each tail hit must compensate for the entire omitted mass, injecting large gradient spikes; even at 98.4% head coverage, a 30K-token reasoning trace contains 460–470 such spikes, thousands per optimizer step (Sec. 4.3.1). Practitioners are thus left choosing between a biased objective and an unstable gradient, which causes sampled-token OPD and HETS to peak early and degrade later in training (Sec. 4.3). To resolve this trade-off, we analyze sparse OPD at the level of the gradient estimator. We first show that any estimator evaluating the head exactly, adding a prefix-dependent control vector weighted by the student’s tail mass, and correcting on tail hits is unbiased for the full-vocabulary rKL gradient, with HETS as the special case of a zero control (Sec. 3.2). Our covariance-trace decomposition reveals that variance splits into an invariant within-tail sampling term and a between-event term proportional to the control’s distance from the true tail mean. This explains HETS’s gradient spikes: its zero control pays for the full tail mean on every hit, whereas any control that better predicts this mean strictly reduces variance. Crucially, the residual masses outside the head define a coarse pseudo-token [3, 12] whose analytical gradient serves as a computable tail-mean predictor. Building on this, we propose On-Policy Distillation with Tail Res idualization (ResOPD): at every step it computes the exact gradient of the (𝑘 + 1) -way coarse distribution, adding only a small residual correction on tail hits (Fig. 2(a)). The resulting estimator is provably unbiased while retaining the same sparse teacher payload. We evaluate ResOPD in two settings. In exact frozen-prefix audits that enumerate every next-token action, ResOPD lowers the gradient covariance trace by 52–75% relative to sampled-token OPD under Top-4 feedback, below both HETS and a payload-matched vOPD baseline (Tab. 2). In end-to-end distillation from Qwen3.5-27B to Qwen3.5-4B, it attains the best average score among the evaluated sparse OPD baselines on four mathematical reasoning benchmarks, 4.40 points above HETS under the same payload (Tab. 3), and its average advantage over HETS consistently holds across every tested support size 𝑘 ∈ { 1, 4, 16 , 32 }, with small margins (Tab. 6). Our contributions are as follows: (1) Bias and variance analysis of sparse OPD. We show that truncated, renormalized, and stopped-coefficient Top-𝑘 rKL objectives are all biased surrogates of the full-vocabulary objective, and we derive a covariance-trace decomposition that separates within-tail sampling variance from between-event variance. This yields the exact condition under which a coarse tail control lowers variance relative to HETS, and a two-control family in which HETS, ResOPD, and the concurrent SC-TA [12] appear as special cases. (2) ResOPD estimator. We propose an estimator that splits the full-vocabulary rKL gradient into an exact (𝑘 + 1) -way coarse term, computed at every step from the Top-𝑘 payload and the residual

# 2. Related Work 

On-Policy Language-Model Distillation. On-policy distillation (OPD) mitigates exposure bias by training students on self-generated rollouts under dense teacher supervision [1, 9]. Subsequent work explores skew divergences [18], adaptive targets [11, 15, 16], and diagnostic behaviors in complex reasoning regimes [8, 32]. However, full-vocabulary OPD incurs prohibitive communication and memory footprints during distributed rollout generation, severely hindering scalability. 

Sparse Divergences and Truncation Biases. To alleviate communication bottlenecks, distributed training frequently adopts sparse Teacher Top-𝑘 payloads [32]. Nonetheless, naively renormalizing or truncating to the Top-𝑘 support optimizes a biased surrogate that misallocates tail probability mass. While coarse-support approaches like TA-OPD [12] incorporate a ghost token [3] to aggregate omitted mass, they still optimize a surrogate (𝑘 + 1) -way divergence that discards the teacher’s fine-grained within-tail geometry. 

Stochastic Estimators and Variance Reduction. An alternative paradigm constructs stochastic estimators to recover the true full-vocabulary rKL gradient unbiasedly. Standard sampled-token estimators [9, 20] suffer from extreme Monte Carlo variance, motivating variance-reduction baselines like vOPD [23]. HETS, a hybrid scheme proposed in EMA-PG [30], computes the Top-𝑘 head analytically and samples only within the tail, yet still incurs severe gradient bursts upon tail hits, which destabilizes training dynamics. 

# 3. Methodology 

In this section, we present the theoretical foundations and algorithmic formulation of On-Policy Distillation with Tail Residualization (ResOPD). We begin in Sec. 3.1 by establishing the full-vocabulary reverse-KL gradient and analyzing the trade-off of sparse teacher payloads, exposing how naive Top-𝑘 heuristics induce objective bias and why hybrid estimators like HETS suffer from tail-sampling variance. In Sec. 3.2, we formulate ResOPD (illustrated in Fig. 2), demonstrating how aggregate tail masses serve as an analytic control variate to achieve an unbiased, variance-reduced estimator. Sec. 3.3 describes the sampling assumptions and connects theoretical covariance bounds to observable online diagnostics and downstream task performance. Finally, Sec. 3.4 extends the analysis to a two-control family.

3.4. A Unified View of Sparse Gradient Estimators 

ResOPD changes the between-event component of the HETS variance while preserving its conditional within-tail randomness. This distinction provides a useful way to compare sparse unbiased estimators: a second, conditionally zero-mean control can act inside the tail. We present the family, its oracle optimum, and the exact conditions governing the ResOPD–SC-TA comparison here. The corresponding audits and downstream results appear in Sec. 4.8.

4.2. Exact frozen-prefix covariance audits 

Tab. 2 reports the exact covariance trace and NSR across five frozen-prefix panels. By analytically enumerating every next-token action at each Student-visited prefix, we isolate the conditional sampling variance of each estimator without decoding noise or optimizer feedback. Under the panel-level aggregation used in the table, both metrics exhibit a consistent ordering across every panel: ResOPD < HETS < vOPD < sampled-token OPD (lower is better). Relative to sampled-token OPD, the aggregate covariance trace is reduced by 7.7% –19 .4% for payload-matched Teacher-Top-𝑘 vOPD, 27 .3% –55 .8% for HETS, and 52 .5% –75 .0% for ResOPD. While Teacher-Top-𝑘 vOPD serves as a competitive baseline in our end-to-end experiments (Tab. 3), its detached scalar control fails to exploit the structural head–tail decomposition, leaving the entire action vector sampled at every step. Consequently, its variance reduction remains modest compared to HETS and ResOPD. Because HETS and ResOPD share the identical payload budget, exact Top-4 head integration, and conditional target structure, their contrast directly isolates the gain from coarse-tail residualization without baseline confounds. We therefore focus our primary ablations on the HETS-to-ResOPD progression. As the support size 𝑘 increases, the exact Top-𝑘 head absorbs more probability mass, causing the residual tail opportunity 𝑃 𝜏 𝑝 to contract. Accordingly, the marginal covariance reduction of ResOPD over HETS gradually narrows. The full support-size sweep in Appendix D.3 tests this communication–variance trade-off directly.

4.7. Learning-rate sensitivity Experimental setup and evaluation protocol. To evaluate optimization robustness under aggressive step sizes, we conduct a controlled learning-rate sweep ( 𝑘 = 4 ) identical to the primary Math experimental setup, except that training is compressed to 1 epoch (64 optimizer steps). 4.8.2. Downstream evaluation comparison 

While SC-TA halves the fixed-prefix residual covariance trace of ResOPD, this advantage does not necessarily translate into superior downstream accuracy. # 5. Conclusion 

We introduced ResOPD, a principled estimator for full-vocabulary reverse-KL distillation under sparse teacher feedback. By integrating the observable coarse distribution analytically and sampling only the unresolved fine-tail residual, ResOPD reduces variance in the evaluated settings while preserving exact unbiasedness under ancestral on-policy sampling, without expanding the teacher payload or adding forward passes. Supported by theoretical analysis, exact covariance audits, and empirical evaluations across math, science, and coding tasks, ResOPD demonstrates higher aggregate downstream scores in the evaluated settings.

C.1. Math OPD experiments 

Fig. 6 illustrates the training trajectories across evaluation suites. On the DeepMath held-out set, ResOPD displays consistent late-stage gains, achieving the highest terminal accuracy among all estimators. Meanwhile, Sampled-token OPD deteriorates severely after an early peak, and HETS suffers a late-stage drop. A similar trend holds on the external math competitions: although TA-OPD maintains strong intermediate trajectory performance and vOPD remains competitive, ResOPD exhibits superior late-stage resilience and decisively surpasses all alternatives at the final step, avoiding the post-peak degradation observed in estimators like Sampled-token OPD and HETS. Together with the lower fixed-prefix covariance trace and improved online training diagnostics reported elsewhere in the paper, these trajectories are consistent with the proposed coarse-tail residualization reducing stochastic gradient noise and improving late-stage optimization stability.

D.2. Finite gradient-enumeration check 

We also verify the unbiasedness identities without Monte Carlo error on a four-action softmax. The Student and Teacher probabilities are respectively (0 .35 , 0.30 , 0.20 , 0.15) and (0 .60 , 0.25 , 0.10 , 0.05) , and the first two actions form the Teacher Top-2 support. We differentiate the full reverse KL directly, enumerate each possible Student action, and weight its estimator gradient by the exact Student action probability.
